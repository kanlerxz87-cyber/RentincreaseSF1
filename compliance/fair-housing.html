Raising Rent on One Unit vs. Both Units — Discrimination & Retaliation Compliance

Objective, neutral decision rules under 42 U.S.C. §3604, California FEHA (§12955), Unruh (§51), Civil Code §1942.5, and SF Administrative Code §37.10B.

Bottom-Line Answer — Should You Raise Rent on One Unit or Both Units?

Raising one unit and not the other is NOT automatically illegal discrimination — Fair Housing laws prohibit why you differentiate (protected characteristics or protected complaints), not objective differences in lease expiration dates, anniversary dates, or ledger histories. However, if both Unit 1 and Unit 2 are legally eligible at the same review cycle, applying the same neutral formula to both units (most conservatively, the routine 1.6% annual CPI allowance) is the cleanest way to prevent disparate-treatment inquiries before they start. If one unit is ineligible (for example, if Unit 1's April 1, 2026 document is a 12-month fixed-term lease), raise only the eligible unit and record the objective contract reason in a dated file memo before serving notice.
