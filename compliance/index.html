Forefront Answer — Must an Individual Owner (Not a Corporation) Get a Rent Board License Before Notifying Tenants?

YES for the SF Rent Board Rent Increase License — NO incorporation required. Under SF Administrative Code §37.15 and SF Rent Board Housing Inventory guidance, every residential owner in San Francisco (including a natural-person owner of a 2-unit 1940 building; 1–9 unit buildings became subject March 1, 2023) must report unit occupancy and rent range annually on the free SF Rent Board Portal (portal.sfrb.org). Reporting a unit as tenant-occupied automatically generates the Rent Increase License at zero cost.

Exact Timing & Challenge-Proof Sequence: The statutory hard bar is having an active license on the effective date of the rent increase (during noncompliance, your license is suspended and suspended months are permanently uncollectible under §37.15(e)). However, because Housing Inventory records are public and tenants can check your status on portal.sfrb.org the day they receive your notice, complete Housing Inventory reporting and save dated PDF/screenshot proof of both active licenses BEFORE serving any notice. You do not send annual/banked notices to the Rent Board for approval, and you do not confuse this license with the separate SF Treasurer Business Registration Certificate (from which a single <4-unit residential building is conditionally exempt). Posted November 9, 2026 — Official SF Rent Board Alert on Proposed File No. 261020 (Pending Committee Action — NOT Law)

Live verification on 2026-11-10 confirms that on November 9, 2026, the SF Rent Board added a formal warning box to Learn About Rent Increases and Banked Rent Increases: Board of Supervisors File No. 261020 (still Pending Committee Action in the Land Use and Transportation Committee as of 2026-11-10 — not enacted law) would cap combined Banked + Capital Improvement increases at 10% per year of the lowest prior-12-month base rent through December 31, 2029 (Legislative Digest Ver. 1). Crucially, if enacted as currently drafted, it would apply retroactively to banked rent increases noticed on or after September 15, 2026 (preserving excess banked % for January 1, 2030+).
