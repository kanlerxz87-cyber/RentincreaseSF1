## FOREFRONT ANSWER — Individual Property Owner: Rent Board License & Pre-Notice Reporting

> **Owner's Core Question:** *"I am just an individual property owner and not part of a corporation, so do I still need to apply for the license and inform the Rent Board before notifying tenants of a rent increase in order to prevent the rent increase from being challenged?"*

### Direct Verified Answer: **YES — Individual Owners Must Obtain the Free Rent Increase License, and Should Do So BEFORE Serving Any Notice**

1. **No Individual-Owner or Small-Building Exemption (`SF Admin. Code § 37.15`):** Under [SF Administrative Code § 37.15(a)–(c), (e)](https://www.sf.gov/information/sec-3715-reporting-obligations-licensing) and [SF Rent Board Housing Inventory guidance](https://www.sf.gov/rent-board-housing-inventory), **every** owner of residential property in San Francisco — individual owners, spouses, family trusts, LLCs, and corporations alike — must report unit data annually into the **Rent Board Housing Inventory** at [portal.sfrb.org](https://portal.sfrb.org/). For condominiums and buildings with **1–9 residential units** (including this 2-unit 1940 building), mandatory reporting began **March 1, 2023**, with annual updates due every **March 1**.
2. **Free & Automatic Online Issuance:** Reporting is **$0 (free)** and available **24/7 online** at [portal.sfrb.org](https://portal.sfrb.org/). Once you submit the required non-owner-occupied unit fields under penalty of perjury, a **Rent Increase License** is **automatically generated** for each tenant-occupied unit.
3. **Timing Nuance — Legal Gate (`Effective Date`) vs. Best-Practice Shield (`Before Serving Notice`):**
   - **Legal enforceability gate (`Rule § 13.14` & `§ 37.15(e)`):** Under [Rent Board Rules & Regulations § 13.14](https://www.sf.gov/reports--december-2022--rent-board-rules-and-regulations-part-xiii-reporting-obligations-under-rent), *"the effective date of the rent increase, not the date of service, shall be used to determine whether or not the increase conforms with the provisions of this section."* If a unit lacks an active Rent Increase License on the **effective date**, the **entire annual/banked rent increase is null and void**, and restoration upon late reporting is **prospective only** ([§ 37.15(e)](https://www.sf.gov/information/sec-3715-reporting-obligations-licensing); note that under [Rule § 13.14](https://www.sf.gov/reports--december-2022--rent-board-rules-and-regulations-part-xiii-reporting-obligations-under-rent), voiding an unlicensed notice does **not** forfeit the owner's underlying banking rights for a future properly licensed notice).
   - **Why you should obtain the license BEFORE serving notice:** Per the Rent Board's [Notice of Owner Noncompliance with Housing Inventory](https://www.sf.gov/notice-of-owner-noncompliance-with-housing-inventory), tenants can look up your unit's license status immediately at [portal.sfrb.org](https://portal.sfrb.org/) by street address or Assessor's Parcel Number (APN). If no license is on file before the effective date, a tenant can file a **Tenant Summary Petition (Form 518)**; if no license is on file on/after the effective date, a tenant can file a **Tenant Petition for Unlawful Rent Increase (Form 516C)** for an Administrative Law Judge hearing and rent refund. Completing the Housing Inventory and downloading both units' license PDFs **before** serving notice prevents both challenges.
4. **Do You File or Pre-Approve the Rent Increase Notice Itself with the Rent Board?**
   - **NO** for standard **annual allowable (`1.6%`)** and **banked** rent increases ([Form 571](https://www.sf.gov/documents/34344/571_Allowable_Annual_Increases_26-27_1.pdf) · [Banked Rent Increases](https://www.sf.gov/information--banked-rent-increases)). You serve the written notice directly on the tenants; you do **not** submit the notice to the Rent Board for approval, nor are you legally required to attach a copy of the license to the notice ([Housing Inventory](https://www.sf.gov/rent-board-housing-inventory)). By contrast, **petition-based increases** (Operating & Maintenance, Capital Improvements, Comparable Rents, Utility Passthroughs) **do** require filing a petition or worksheet with the Rent Board **before** serving notice ([Landlord Petitions & Passthroughs](https://www.sf.gov/landlord-petitions-and-passthroughs)).

### 2. Corporate REITs vs. Individual SF Property Owners

- **Identical Legal Rules:** Both corporate REITs and individual 2-unit owners face the exact same `§ 37.15` Rent Increase License requirement, `1.6%` Form 571 cap, and `Civ. Code § 827` service rules ([Admin. Code § 37.15](https://www.sf.gov/information/sec-3715-reporting-obligations-licensing)).
- **Empirical Structural Differences ([2024 Census/HUD RHFS](https://www.census.gov/content/dam/Census/programs-surveys/rhfs/2024-rhfs-infographic.pdf)):** Nationally, `2–4` unit properties represent `14.6%` of rental properties (`15.5%` of units) with median DSCR `74` and mean operating cost `$5,820/unit`, whereas `50+` unit properties represent `0.4%` of properties (`39.2%` of units) with median DSCR `164` and `$10,979/unit` operating cost. On [portal.sfrb.org](https://www.sf.gov/rent-board-housing-inventory), `5+` unit portfolios may use bulk Excel uploads, whereas `1–4` unit owners are directed to the Individual Unit form and enjoy the conditional `<4`-unit Business Registration carve-out ([SF Treasurer](https://sftreasurer.org/information-lessors-residential-real-estate)).
- **Algorithmic Pricing Ban (`SF Admin. Code § 37.10C` & `DOJ v. RealPage`):** San Francisco bans algorithmic revenue-management software using nonpublic competitor data (`$1,000/month/unit` penalties + attorney's fees + nonprofit standing under [§ 37.10C](https://www.sf.gov/information--sec-3710c-use-and-sale-algorithmic-devices-prohibited); see also [DOJ Nov 24, 2025 RealPage proposed consent judgment](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/justice-department-requires-realpage-end-sharing-competitively-sensitive-information-and)). Individual owners using published Form 571 percentages and manual unit ledgers have zero `§ 37.10C` exposure. No official SF dataset measures behavioral annual-vs.-banking frequency by corporate vs. individual owners; that metric is left strictly blank (`FL-15`).

### 3. Proper Tenant Notification & Lawful Reasons

- **Mandatory Service (`Cal. Civ. Code § 827(b)` & `CCP § 1013(a)`):** Written notice served **personally** (`30` days for `<=10%`, `90` days for `>10%`) or by **USPS first-class mail** (`+5` calendar days within CA = `35` / `95` days, with date/place of mailing notation per `CCP § 1013(b)`). Email/text/portal-only or "nail-and-mail" alone does **not** satisfy `§ 827(b)(1)`.
- **Lawful Reasons:** Annual CPI (`1.6%`) and properly banked increases are **statutory entitlements** under `§ 37.3(a)(1)–(2)` requiring **no cost excuse** in a cover letter. Stating informal excuses like *"higher property taxes / mortgage"* without an approved O&M petition (`Rule § 6.10`, which bars debt service and reassessment taxes for post-April 3, 2018 purchases!), *"extra roommate"* (`§ 37.3(a)(11)` illegal), or *"below market rent"* (`§ 37.3` illegal without a Comparable Rents petition) invites immediate Rent Board challenge ([Landlord Petitions](https://www.sf.gov/landlord-petitions-and-passthroughs)).

### 5. Raising One Unit vs. Both Units (Fair Housing & Anti-Discrimination Analysis)

- **Legal Rule:** Raising rent on **one unit** and not the other is **not automatically illegal** if driven by objective, documented, non-discriminatory facts — for example, if **Unit 1** is locked into a fixed-term lease from `April 1, 2026` while **Unit 2** is month-to-month with no increase since `2019`. Never serve an unlawful mid-lease increase on Unit 1 merely for "symmetry."
- **Why Applying One Uniform Written Policy to Both Units Is Safest:** Once **both** units are legally eligible, applying a single neutral written policy (e.g., imposing the published `1.6%` annual CPI allowance on every eligible month-to-month unit on its next eligible date, or applying a uniform written banking formula across the building) eliminates any pretext or disparate-treatment inference under **California FEHA ([Gov. Code § 12955](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=GOV&sectionNum=12955))**, the **Unruh Civil Rights Act ([Civ. Code § 51](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=51))**, the **Federal Fair Housing Act ([42 U.S.C. § 3604](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2023-title42/html/USCODE-2023-title42-chap45-subchapI-sec3604.htm))**, and **SF Tenant Harassment ([Admin. Code § 37.10B](https://www.sf.gov/information--sec-3710b-tenant-harassment))**.
- **Critical Trap for 2-Unit Individual Owners:** Even if an individual owner lived on-site (where federal `42 U.S.C. § 3603(b)(2)` has a partial "Mrs. Murphy" exemption for 1–4 unit owner-occupied buildings, though never for `§ 3604(c)` statements), **California FEHA (`Gov. Code § 12955`), California Unruh (`Civ. Code § 51`), and SF `§ 37.10B` have NO 2-unit landlord exemption**! Every unit-selection decision must be 100% neutral and documented in writing before serving notice.
