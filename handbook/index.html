What an individual owner must do before any rent increase

Start with the licence answer, then work through calculations, notice, fair housing and the tenant-lawyer audit. Every table row links to its official source.

Forefront answer: yes — an individual owner needs the Rent Increase License, obtained before serving notice

There is no individual-owner or small-building exemption from Housing Inventory reporting. Every owner of a 1–9 unit building (including this reported two-unit building) has had to report annually since March 1, 2023. Reporting is free at portal.sfrb.org; a Rent Increase License is then generated for each tenant-occupied unit. The legal test is licence-on-the-effective-date, but tenants can look the licence up immediately and petition before (Form 518) or after (Form 516C) the effective date — so the challenge-proof workflow is: report first, download both licences, then serve notice. Ordinary annual/banked notices themselves are served on tenants, not filed with the Rent Board for approval.

Legislative alert: pending File #261020 (not law)

Live Legistar check on 2026-11-11: Pending Committee Action, no final action or enactment date. As proposed (Leg Ver1 / Leg Dig Ver1) it would cap combined banked + capital-improvement increases at 10% of the lowest base rent in the prior 12 months through December 31, 2029, for banked notices on or after September 15, 2026, preserving excess for January 1, 2030 or later. Any banked notice served now is after that proposed cutoff. Unit 2 therefore also shows partial-banked options U2-C1 and U2-C2. Re-check before serving.
