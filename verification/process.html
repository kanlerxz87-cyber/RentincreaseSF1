Question 1 of 8 · the licence and the Board

The Rent Board process, step by step

Eight steps from confirming coverage to keeping proof of service. The licence is created by the annual report, so the work happens before the notice and the notice is never filed with the Board.

The licence, preciselyBecause this is the part the owner's premise gets almost right.

You do not apply for permission for each increase. You keep a licence current by reporting once a year.

Two separate duties are often confused. The reporting duty is annual and Board-facing: it is the Housing Inventory. The licence is the automatic result of that reporting for a tenant-occupied unit. The notice is tenant-facing and is not filed with the Board.

What that means in practice: the compliance work happens before the notice and does not mention the notice. If the report is already current for both units, an ordinary 1.6% increase needs no additional Board step at all.

Eight steps, in orderSteps 1 and 2 decide whether the arithmetic is even usable. Steps 5 and 6 are where most defects occur.

1Confirm the unit is covered by the Rent Ordinance
Check the year built and the unit type on the City's Property Information Map, and record the certificate-of-occupancy date. Units built on or before 13 June 1979 that are not single-family dwellings are generally covered.
If a unit is genuinely exempt, the local cap and the licence do not apply and the whole analysis changes.Owner must confirmS18
2Report each unit in the Rent Board Housing Inventory
Report online through the Rent Board Portal: owner contact details, approximate square footage, bedrooms, bathrooms, occupancy date, and base rent in $250 increments. Reporting is free. The annual deadline is 1 March; for buildings with fewer than 10 units reporting began 1 March 2023. Late filing is accepted and the Board states it does not assess a monetary penalty for filing late.
This report is the thing that creates the licence. It is also the only Board-facing filing in the ordinary annual/banked route.Required every yearS01 S03
3Confirm a current rent increase licence exists for each unit
Once the report is submitted, a licence is generated automatically for a non-owner-occupied unit. Confirm it in the Portal and keep a dated record. Best practice is to do this before serving any notice.
Without a current licence the increase cannot take effect, and the tenant does not owe the increase for the months the licence was suspended.Owner must confirmS01 S03
4Fix the current base rent and the anniversary date from the records
Read the signed lease and every amendment, then the full rent ledger. Base rent includes housing services such as parking or storage and excludes temporary passthroughs. For a fixed term, rent cannot be raised during the term unless the agreement expressly allows it.
The percentage applies to base rent, and the first increase is only available 12 months after the tenancy began.Owner must supply recordsS05 S20 S02
5Calculate the increase additively
Multiply base rent by the percentage in force on the effective date (1.6% for increases effective 1 Mar 2026 – 28 Feb 2027). Any properly banked percentages are added to that figure, never compounded and never rounded up. A landlord may take less than the maximum.
Compounding or rounding up produces an amount that exceeds the published allowance.CalculatedS06 S05 S04 S07
6Write the notice with the required itemisation
State the dollar amount, the percentage, the effective date, which portion is the annual increase and which portion (if any) is banked, and the dates the banked amount is based on.
Section 37.3(b) requires the itemisation, and Rule 4.12(b) requires the banked basis dates; Rule 4.10(b) and Rule 4.12(b) contain limited exceptions, so the safe course is to include everything.RequiredS02 S07 S05
7Serve the notice with enough lead time
30 days' written notice if the increase is 10% or less; 90 days if it is more than 10%, whether alone or combined with other increases in the 12 months before the effective date. Serve personally or by mail under Code of Civil Procedure §1013 — mail inside California adds 5 calendar days (10 if outside California, 20 if outside the United States). Keep proof of service.
A short notice is a defect the tenant can raise, regardless of whether the arithmetic is right.CalculatedS08 S21 S05

Review the repair and complaint history, any protected activity, and the basis for selecting which units to increase. Keep the final notice and the service evidence. Re-check the licence on the effective date.

Two things that are easy to get wrong

1. The rate is chosen by the effective date, not the notice date. A notice served in December 2026 for a February 2027 increase uses the 1.6% that is in force in February 2027. If the increase were delayed into March 2027, the percentage would be whatever the Board publishes for the new year — which is not yet published. S05 S06

2. The behaviour of the occupant change matters more than the percentage. A partial change — one original tenant stays — does not reset the rent. A full turnover can. Between those two answers is a different legal route, and it turns on who actually remained. S11
