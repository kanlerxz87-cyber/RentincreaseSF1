Question by question

Your eight questions, answered from the text

Each answer is limited to what the official sources actually say. Where a question cannot be answered from an official source, that is stated rather than filled in.

Do I have to inform the Rent Board and get a licence before I notify tenants of a rent increase?Answer, then the official basis.

Yes to the licence, no to per-increase notification. As a single individual owner you must report each tenant-occupied unit in the annual Housing Inventory, which then generates a free rent increase licence. The licence must be current when the increase takes effect. You do not send the notice to the Board for approval and you do not file anything about the specific increase.

Does being an individual rather than a corporation change anything?Answer, then the official basis.

No. The duty is written for “owners of residential dwelling units subject to this Chapter 37”. Small buildings simply began reporting later — 1 March 2023 rather than 1 July 2022 — and there is no individual-owner, two-unit or small-portfolio exemption in the text.

What is a reasonable and normal increase in 94122?Answer, then the official basis.

The only officially verified figure is the published annual allowance: 1.6% for increases taking effect between 1 March 2026 and 28 February 2027. No official source publishes a market “norm” for this ZIP, that cell stays blank, and commercial listings were not used. Properly banked amounts can be added on top, but only where the records support them.

S06 S05

What do local, state and federal law each require here?Answer, then the official basis.

Local: the Rent Ordinance supplies the cap, the licence, the banking rules and the notice itemisation. State: Civil Code §827 supplies the 30/90-day notice and service methods, and the AB 1482 cap does not apply to units already under the tighter local cap. Federal: the Fair Housing Act governs discriminatory terms; no general federal percentage cap for private unsubsidised housing was located in this review.

S02 S08 S09 S10 S14

How do large corporations and individual owners handle increases?Answer, then the official basis.

The same San Francisco duties bind both, so the mechanics are identical. The only official comparative material located concerns pricing software: San Francisco bans algorithmic devices that use non-public competitor data, and the DOJ's RealPage matter is a proposed settlement based on allegations. No official dataset comparing individual and corporate behaviour was found, so that comparison is left blank.

S02 S16 S22

What are reasonable reasons for an increase, and how should tenants be told?Answer, then the official basis.

The defensible basis is the published allowance and proven banked amounts, expressed in a written notice that states the dollar amount, the percentage, the effective date and the banked portions and dates. Market catch-up, a new roommate, or pressure to vacate are not lawful bases for this kind of increase. Tenants are told by served written notice, not by a filing.

S05 S02 S07 S08

Should I raise both units to avoid a discrimination claim?Answer, then the official basis.

Raising only one unit is not automatically discrimination, and raising both is not a safe harbour. What matters is why a unit was selected: choosing on a protected characteristic is unlawful, and so is bad-faith pressure. Apply a neutral, documented method to each unit, and do not raise a unit that is not eligible merely for symmetry.
