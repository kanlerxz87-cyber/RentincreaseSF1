Q-01 Is a current Rent Increase Licence on file for each unit? Without it the entire increase is null and void on the effective date Search the Rent Board Portal by address or Assessor's Parcel Number; check each relevant year; screenshot the result S02 S08
Q-02 Has the annual Housing Inventory been filed for each unit, for each year since 1 March 2023? Reporting is what generates the licence; a gap suspends the licence for that period Portal submission history; if nothing is on file, submit now (free, and no late penalty) S02 S01
Q-03 What is Unit A's rent-increase anniversary date — 1 February or 1 April? It sets the applicable rate and the notice deadline Read the 2025-02-01 agreement and the 2026-04-01 document; if still ambiguous, ask a Rent Board counsellor S03
Q-04 What is Unit B's current base rent? Every dollar figure on this site uses the reported 2019 starting amount Reconcile the rent ledger and bank statements from 2019-06-01 to date S03
Q-05 Has Unit B's rent truly been unchanged since 2019-06-01? The 13.1% banked figure assumes no increase was ever imposed The ledger, plus every notice ever served S04 S05
Q-06 Are both original 2019 occupants still in possession of Unit B? Banked increases do not carry over to a new tenant Occupancy records and the Housing Inventory 12-month occupancy history S04
Q-07 Is either unit officially covered by SF rent control? The whole analysis assumes coverage Official Property Information Map for the build date; Rent Board counselling on exemptions (415-252-4600) S11 S13
Q-08 Was a written §6.14 notice served on the incoming Unit A occupant? It decides whether a market reset is available if the last original occupant later leaves The owner's own files; if none exists, note it now and serve one going forward S10
Q-09 Has either household made a habitability complaint, contacted an agency, or been part of an inspection in the last 180 days? A rent increase is barred for 180 days after such an event Repair requests, correspondence, agency notices S16
Q-10 What is the annual rate from 1 March 2027? It is the annual component for Unit B's 2027-06-01 date and Unit A's 2027-04-01 scenario The Rent Board publishes it on 1 March 2027; it cannot be known before then S03 S13
Q-11 What will File No. 261020 do, and when? It may cap Unit B's banked catch-up at 10% per year Legistar File No. 261020; the Rent Board's posted notice; re-check before serving anything large S20 S07

Q-13 What is the base-rent composition — are there any pass-throughs? Pass-throughs must be excluded from the base used for the percentage The lease and any pass-through notices S03 S05
Q-14 Is a Business Registration Certificate required? Separate from the licence and does not affect lawfulness, but penalties can accrue Office of the Treasurer & Tax Collector guidance S01
Q-15 What counts as ‘reasonable and normal’ in 94122? It is the question asked, but no official body publishes a ZIP-level norm Unanswerable from official sources. The lawful maximum is published; a market norm is not. Left blank by design S03 S06

The decision rule this page implies

Q-01, Q-02, Q-03, Q-04 and Q-05 are hard blockers. Until the licence status is confirmed, Unit A's anniversary is fixed, and Unit B's ledger is reconciled, the dollar amounts on this site are arithmetic exercises and nothing more. They must not be used to draft a notice.

Q-11 is a timing blocker for Unit B only. A banked catch-up above 10% served while File No. 261020 is pending and drafted to reach notices served on or after 15 September 2026 is a decision that should be made consciously, with advice.

Q-15 and Q-16 are permanently blank by design. They are the two questions where an answer would have to be invented, and inventing one is exactly what this site is built not to do.
