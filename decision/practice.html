The law does not care whether you are a corporation

Section 37.15 says “Owners.” The only officially published difference between a large portfolio and a two-unit building is when the duty started — not what it requires.

What this means for you as a single-building owner

Three points follow directly from the official text:

  1. You are not exempt and you are not a special case. The ordinance applies to “Owners” with no threshold. If it helps: the Rent Board has said most owners must report “even if you are not a landlord”.
  2. You have one advantage, not a legal one but a practical one. With two units you can evidence your decision unit-by-unit from two short ledgers. A 200-unit owner cannot explain any single increase without a system. Documented, neutral, unit-level arithmetic is the strongest record available to you and it is cheap to produce.
  3. You carry the same exposure as a corporation. 42 U.S.C. §3604 and Government Code §12955 apply to the owner of any housing accommodation regardless of portfolio size. There is no small-landlord carve-out.
