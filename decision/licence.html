Licence & the Rent Board

It is a City ordinance, and there is no application form

You do not apply for approval and you do not send the Rent Board your rent notice. You report each unit once a year into the Housing Inventory, and the licence for a tenant-occupied unit is generated automatically.

Local law — S.F. Admin. Code §37.15Fewer than 10 units: from 1 Mar 2023Free to reportLicence tested on the effective date

The one-sentence answer

The 2023 rule is local — San Francisco Administrative Code Chapter 37, §37.15, added by Ordinance No. 265-20, effective 18 January 2021, with the duty reaching condominiums and buildings with fewer than 10 units on 1 March 2023. It is not a state law. The state law you must also obey is the notice rule in Civil Code §827.

How a tenant — or a tenant's lawyer — will test you

The Rent Board publishes the exact route a tenant uses, and it takes about two minutes. See F-17.

  1. Search the Rent Board Portal by address or Assessor's Parcel Number.
  2. Check whether a Rent Increase Licence existed on the effective date of the increase — the Board's guidance says to “check multiple years for all relevant dates”.
  3. If there was no licence and the increase has not yet taken effect: file a Tenant Summary Petition (Form 518). The Board then sends the landlord a letter saying a licence must be obtained before the increase takes effect.
  4. If it has already taken effect: file a Tenant Petition for Unlawful Rent Increase (Form 516C), which may be set for a hearing before an Administrative Law Judge and can lead to a rent refund.

The licence does not have to be attached to your notice — but that does not help you, because the tenant can look it up anyway.
