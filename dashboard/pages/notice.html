Notice & Reasons

The correct way to inform tenants, required notice contents, and official grounds for a rent increase.

What "reasonable reasons" means in official sources

For the ordinary annual allowable increase, official SF.gov pages describe a percentage-based entitlement for a licensed landlord and do not list a separate justification requirement; the six categories that require a petition and supporting documentation are listed separately. Because no official page explicitly says "no reason is required", that negative inference is flagged rather than asserted as verified (F09). S01 S13 S14

General "market conditions" or "inflation" are not listed as standalone grounds for an above-AGA increase to an existing tenancy; the official grounds are the petition categories below. S14 S15 S09

Correct notification procedure

Step-by-step notice procedure
StepRequired actionOfficial source
1Confirm the unit is covered by SF rent control and check exemptions.S07 S27
2Obtain / verify a current rent increase license through the Housing Inventory before the increase takes effect.S13 S01
3Determine the lawful base rent, the 12-month anniversary date, and any banked amount from the full rent history.S01 S05 S18
4Use the percentage in effect on the effective date (1.6% for March 1, 2026 – February 28, 2027).S01 S02
5Calculate on base rent only; do not compound; do not round up to the nearest dollar.S01 S06
6Serve written notice at least 30 days before the effective date (90 days if the increase alone or combined is over 10%; add 5 days if mailed).S01 S29
7Include the dollar amount, percentage, effective date, and which portion is annual, banked, or a certified passthrough.S01 S09 S05
8For petition-based increases, file the Rent Board petition before serving the notice; tenants need not pay until approved.S14 S15 S16
9Keep a copy of the notice, proof of service, license, and calculation for tenant-lawyer review.S13 S18

Common notice mistake

Form 1007 ("Notice to Tenant Required by §37.9(c)") is officially required on notices to terminate tenancy, not on rent-increase notices. Rent-increase notice content comes from §37.3(b), Rules §4.10, Civil Code §827, and SF.gov guidance. S24 S09 S12 S29 See F12.

Service detail

Civil Code §827 requires written notice delivered personally or served by mail under Code of Civil Procedure §1013; SF.gov adds 5 days when the notice is mailed. S29 S01
