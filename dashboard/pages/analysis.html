1. What is a reasonable and normal increase under local, state, and federal law?

Official sources do not define "reasonable" or "normal" as a separate legal standard; they publish a maximum annual allowable increase. For a covered, licensed unit, the ordinary business practice documented by the Rent Board is one increase of up to 1.6% on the tenant's anniversary, calculated on base rent only, with no rounding up. S01 S02 S13 An owner may impose less, and nothing in the Rent Board rules requires any landlord to raise any tenant's rent. S05 S12

The state cap is higher, but official guidance says the more protective local rule applies. S27 S28 For federal law, no numeric rent-increase cap for private unsubsidized housing was confirmed from an official source; HUD describes rent controls as state and local actions and enforces the Fair Housing Act. That value is therefore blank and flagged rather than inferred. S39 S37 S38

3. Findings that a tenant lawyer would scrutinize

  • Unit A occupancy: a new roommate cannot itself trigger extra rent; a co-tenant is not subject to a Costa-Hawkins increase; §1954.53(d)(3) excludes partial occupancy changes where an original occupant remains. S21 S20 S32
  • Unit A timing: a skipped increase is only banked after the applicable 24-month condition, and changing the anniversary date can lose an increase. S05 S06
  • Unit B banking: increases cannot be compounded; the sum must be tied to the correct anniversary dates and full rent history. S06 S05 S18
  • Notice threshold: any combined increase above 10% needs 90 days' written notice. S01 S29
  • Above-AGA increases: six petition categories require Rent Board forms, supporting documentation, and prior filing; hardship relief is not available for annual or banked increases. S14 S17

Recommended decision path (analysis, not legal advice)

Confirm coverage and license; obtain the full rent history for both units; calculate each unit separately from its own lawful base rent and anniversary; choose a documented uniform policy; serve the correct written notice; then have an SF tenant lawyer review the complete file. S07 S13 S18 S01 S22
