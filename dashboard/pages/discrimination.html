One Unit or Both

Whether to increase one or both units, reviewed against federal, state, and local anti-discrimination law.

The question

Would it be acceptable to raise the rent on one tenant's unit, or does it make more sense to raise both to avoid discrimination lawsuits or inquiries? No official source was found that requires both units to be raised or that treats a both-unit increase as a legal safe harbor — that gap is flagged as F08. S37 S33 S38 S11

What official sources do establish is that rent increases are calculated per unit and per tenancy (base rent, anniversary, banking, license), and that discrimination in the terms and conditions of rental is prohibited. S01 S09 S13 S37

Legal framework

Recommended, reviewable approach

Write down one uniform, objective policy before acting: apply the published AGA to the lawful base rent on each unit's own anniversary, use the same notice form and service method, document the license and calculation for both units, and record a legitimate nondiscriminatory business reason (e.g., keeping pace with the officially published allowable increase). Then have an SF tenant lawyer review both files together. Uniformity is a risk-control measure, not a verified legal immunity. S01 S13 S09 S33 S22

Why raising only one unit can still be defensible

The two units have different user-supplied start dates and rents (Unit A: February 1, 2025 / $3,995; Unit B: June 1, 2019 / $3,150). Different anniversary dates and different banking histories are objective, non-personal reasons for different timing. Those inputs are unverified, so an SF tenant lawyer should confirm the rent history before relying on them. S01 S05 S18 See F05.

Disparate-impact caution

Even a neutral policy can create liability under 24 CFR §100.500 if it has a discriminatory effect without a legally sufficient justification; state protected classes are broader than the federal list. S38 S33
