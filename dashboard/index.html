Overview

A clean, source-linked dashboard for a privacy-first review of rent increases on two rent-controlled San Francisco units.

What this dashboard answers

Whether a rent increase for two rent-controlled units in a San Francisco 94122-area building would be normal and lawful under local, state, and federal law; how notice must be given; how corporate and individual owners are treated; and whether one or both units should be increased.

Every published value was read from an official government page. Anything not confirmed from an official source is left blank and listed on the Flagged for Review page.

Privacy rule

No real names, initials, street addresses, or contact details are published. Only placeholders (Unit A, Unit B, Tenant A1…, Tenant B1…) are used. The user-supplied dates and amounts are kept verbatim but are flagged as unverified and are never presented as official records.

The Rent Board also notes that Housing Inventory submissions are public; tenant identities are deliberately kept out of this public site. S13

Answers at a glance

  • Normal local increase: the published annual allowable increase of 1.6%, once every 12 months, on base rent only, for a licensed landlord. S01 S13
  • State law: AB 1482's 8.8% cap does not replace the more protective SF cap for units covered by the Rent Ordinance. S27 S28
  • Federal law: no numeric rent-increase cap for private unsubsidized housing was confirmed; federal fair-housing law still applies. That value is left blank and flagged. S39 S37 S38
  • Corporate vs individual owners: official sources document enforcement against shared pricing algorithms and rules that apply to all owners; no official comparative practice dataset was found. S35 S36 S10 S13
  • One unit or both: no official rule requires both units to be raised, and no official source supports a discrimination "safe harbor"; a uniform, documented, objective policy is the recommended analysis. S37 S33 S12
