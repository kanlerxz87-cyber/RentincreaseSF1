## 1. FOREFRONT ANSWER: Do Individual Property Owners Need a Rent Board License & Do You Inform the Rent Board First?

### Direct Answer for Individual ("Mom-and-Pop") Owners

1. **SF Rent Board "Rent Increase License" (via Housing Inventory, Admin. Code § 37.15): YES — MANDATORY.**
   - Even though you own the property as an **individual** and **not** as a corporation, LLC, or real estate company, San Francisco Administrative Code **§ 37.15** contains **zero exemption** for individual or small-building owners ([SF Admin. Code § 37.15](https://www.sf.gov/information/sec-3715-reporting-obligations-licensing)).
   - Residential buildings with **1–9 units** became subject to mandatory annual Housing Inventory reporting on **March 1, 2023**, with annual updates due **March 1** every year ([SF Rent Board Housing Inventory](https://www.sf.gov/rent-board-housing-inventory)).
   - Reporting is **$0 / completely free**, takes ~10–15 minutes online 24/7 at the [SF Rent Board Portal (`portal.sfrb.org`)](https://portal.sfrb.org/), and **automatically generates** a Rent Increase License for each unit reported as tenant-occupied ("Non-Owner Occupied").

2. **Timing — Must You Inform the Rent Board BEFORE Serving Tenants?**
   - **Strict Statutory Gate (`Effective Date`):** Under § 37.15(e) and official SF Rent Board guidance, your rent increase is unenforceable unless you hold a current Rent Increase License **on the effective date** of the increase. During any period of non-compliance, your right to raise rent is suspended; once you report, the license is restored **prospectively only**, and tenants do **not** owe increased rent for any month during suspension ([SF Admin. Code § 37.15(e)](https://www.sf.gov/information/sec-3715-reporting-obligations-licensing); [Housing Inventory](https://www.sf.gov/rent-board-housing-inventory)).
   - **Practical Best-Practice Sequence (`BEFORE Serving Notice`):** Because tenants and free San Francisco tenant attorneys can search `portal.sfrb.org` the same day they receive your rent increase notice to see whether your unit has an active license, **always submit your Housing Inventory and save/download both units' active Rent Increase License PDFs BEFORE serving any rent increase notice** ([Rent Board Portal](https://portal.sfrb.org/)).

3. **Do You File the Rent Increase Notice Itself with the Rent Board?**
   - **NO.** Once your Housing Inventory is submitted and your Rent Increase License is active on `portal.sfrb.org`, standard annual allowable (`1.6%`) and banked (`Rule 4.12`) rent increase notices are served **directly on the tenants** — you do **not** submit the notice to the Rent Board for pre-approval, and you do **not** need to attach a copy of the license to the notice ([Housing Inventory](https://www.sf.gov/rent-board-housing-inventory); [Learn About Rent Increases](https://www.sf.gov/learn-about-rent-increases-san-francisco)).

4. **Don't Confuse the Three San Francisco Registrations / Fees:**

## 2. Beginner-Friendly Step-by-Step Walkthrough (Zero Prior Rent Board Experience Needed)

1. **Step 1 — Gather Non-Sensitive Unit Facts (5 Minutes):** For Unit 1 and Unit 2, write down: (a) owner or designated habitability contact name, business mailing address, phone, and email; (b) Business Registration Number *"if any"* (leave blank if exempt under the `<4-unit` rule above); (c) approximate unit square footage in `250 sq. ft.` increments (good-faith estimate permitted; check [SF Property Information Map](https://sfplanninggis.org/PIM/)); (d) bedroom and bathroom count; (e) occupancy start date (`Feb 1, 2025` for Unit 1; `Jun 1, 2019` for Unit 2); and (f) current base rent in `$250` increments plus which utilities (water/sewer, refuse, gas, electricity) are included in base rent ([Housing Inventory](https://www.sf.gov/rent-board-housing-inventory)).
2. **Step 2 — Create Your Owner Account on the Official Portal:** Visit <https://portal.sfrb.org/> and click **Create an Account** under *Property Owners/Managers*. Link your parcel using the PIN on your mailed Rent Board notice (or call `311` / `415-701-2311` for PIN assistance). Use the **Individual Unit** web form — ignore the 5+ unit Excel template ([Rent Board Portal](https://portal.sfrb.org/)).
3. **Step 3 — Submit the Housing Inventory for Both Units:** Select Unit 1 -> mark **Non-Owner Occupied** -> enter the Step 1 details -> submit under penalty of perjury. Repeat for Unit 2 ([Admin. Code § 37.15](https://www.sf.gov/information/sec-3715-reporting-obligations-licensing)).
4. **Step 4 — Download Both Rent Increase Licenses & Check the $59/Unit Fee:** Immediately download/screenshot the generated **Rent Increase License** for Unit 1 and Unit 2, and confirm your `$59.00/unit` Rent Board Fee is paid in full ([Rent Board Fee](https://www.sf.gov/rent-board-fee)).
5. **Step 5 — Audit Leases, Ledgers, Repairs & Pending File #261020:** Read Unit 1's April 1, 2026 document (confirm month-to-month vs. fixed-term), verify Unit 2's full ledger since June 1, 2019, confirm zero open DBI violations or uncompleted tenant repair requests, and check pending Board of Supervisors [File #261020](https://sfgov.legistar.com/LegislationDetail.aspx?ID=8217304&GUID=7EDA4662-EBFB-4AF5-962F-C669B64AA770) ([CA Tenants Guide](https://www4.courts.ca.gov/documents/California-Tenants-Guide.pdf); [Civ. Code § 1942.4](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1942.4)).
6. **Step 6 — Calculate Exact Base-Rent Increase (Add, Never Compound, Never Round Up):** Multiply verified base rent by the [Form 571](https://www.sf.gov/documents/34344/571_Allowable_Annual_Increases_26-27_1.pdf) percentage in effect on the **effective date** (`1.6%` for `March 1, 2026 – February 28, 2027`).
7. **Step 7 — Serve Written Itemized Notice Properly:** Deliver a written notice to all adult tenants via **personal hand delivery** (`30` days if `<=10%`; `90` days if `>10%`) OR **USPS First-Class Mail** (`+5` calendar days in California = `35` days if `<=10%`; `95` days if `>10%`, with date and place of mailing noted per [CCP § 1013(b)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=1013&lawCode=CCP)). **Never rely on email, text, or door-posting alone** ([Civ. Code § 827](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=827)).
8. **Step 8 — Renew Housing Inventory Annually by March 1:** Renew on `portal.sfrb.org` every year before March 1 so your license remains continuously active on and after the effective date ([Housing Inventory](https://www.sf.gov/rent-board-housing-inventory)).

## 5. Answers to Key Legal & Practical Inquiries

1. **Local, State & Federal Laws (`94122` Outer Sunset):**
   - **Local (`Controlling`):** SF Rent Ordinance (`S.F. Admin. Code Ch. 37`) limits annual increases to `1.6%` (`3/1/2026–2/28/2027`) plus proven non-compounded banked increases (`Rule 4.12`), conditioned on an active `§ 37.15` license. No official government dataset publishes a "normal market rent increase" specific to ZIP `94122` (left blank rather than inferred from commercial listing sites).
   - **State (`Preempted Cap, Controlling Procedure`):** California's AB 1482 cap (`8.8%` in SF for `8/1/2026–7/31/2027`) **does not apply** because `Cal. Civ. Code § 1947.12(d)(3)` exempts units covered by SF's stricter `1.6%` local cap ([SF AB 1482 Report](https://www.sf.gov/reports--california-tenant-protection-act-2019-ab-1482)). State law controls notice delivery and lead times (`Civ. Code § 827`, `CCP § 1013`), habitability bars (`Civ. Code § 1942.4`), retaliation (`Civ. Code § 1942.5`), and fair housing (`Gov. Code § 12955`, `Civ. Code § 51`).
   - **Federal:** The Fair Housing Act (`42 U.S.C. § 3604`) prohibits discriminatory rental terms; no general federal rent-cap statute governs unsubsidized private rentals ([42 U.S.C. § 3604](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2023-title42/html/USCODE-2023-title42-chap45-subchapI-sec3604.htm)).

2. **How Big Real Estate Corporations vs. Individual SF Owners Handle Increases:**
   - Federal survey data ([2024 HUD/Census RHFS](https://www.census.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026/2024-rhfs-data.html)) confirms institutional REITs/LLCs concentrate in large multifamily assets with automated annual CPI notice calendars and dedicated March 1 portal compliance, whereas 2–4 unit owners are predominantly individuals who often skip increases for years and risk licensing or notice-timing mistakes when catching up.
   - In San Francisco, **both** corporate and individual landlords are strictly bound by **SF Admin. Code § 37.10C**, which bans algorithmic revenue-management software using nonpublic competitor data (`$1,000/month/unit` civil penalty + tenant attorney's fees; compare [U.S. DOJ RealPage proposed settlement](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/justice-department-requires-realpage-end-sharing-competitively-sensitive-information-and)) ([SF § 37.10C](https://www.sf.gov/information--sec-3710c-use-and-sale-algorithmic-devices-prohibited)).

3. **Correct Way to Inform Tenants & Reasonable Reasons:**
   - Serve a clean written notice via **personal delivery** or **USPS First-Class Mail** (`+5` days in CA) stating old base rent, exact dollar increase, exact percentage, new base rent, effective date, itemized annual vs. banked breakdown with banked anniversary dates (`Rule 4.10(a)` / `Rule 4.12(b)`), and mailing date/place notation (`CCP § 1013(b)`).
   - No hardship narrative is required. Neutral, standard business phrasing (*"Annual allowable rent adjustment pursuant to SF Admin. Code § 37.3(a) and Form 571 to offset standard operating, property tax, insurance, and maintenance costs"*) satisfies `Civ. Code § 1942.5(f)–(g)` without triggering harassment or roommate-surcharge violations.

4. **Passing SF Tenant-Lawyer Scrutiny & Raising One Unit vs. Both Units:**
   - Free and low-cost SF tenant counselors/legal aid organizations (such as Open Door Legal's Sunset office at `1722 Irving St, SF 94122`, Housing Rights Committee of SF, SF Tenants Union, and Bay Area Legal Aid — subject to intake/eligibility rules; tenants may also petition the Rent Board without a lawyer per [Rent Board Referral Listing](https://www.sf.gov/reports--january-2025--rent-board-referral-listing)) check 15 statutory gates: coverage, active `§ 37.15` license, fixed-term lease bar, 12-month spacing, base-rent purity, `Form 571` window, exact unrounded math, banking rules & File #261020, itemization, `§ 6.14` compliance, `Civ. Code § 827`/`CCP § 1013` service, `Civ. Code § 1942.4` citations, `Form 516B` open repairs, `Civ. Code § 1942.5` 180-day retaliation, and Fair Housing neutrality.
   - **One Unit vs. Both Units:** Raising only one unit is **not automatically illegal** if grounded in objective contract facts (for example, if Unit 1 is locked in a fixed-term lease through March 31, 2027 while Unit 2 is month-to-month). However, when **both** units are legally eligible, applying **one uniform, neutral building-wide policy to both units** is the strongest defense against any disparate-treatment inquiry under FEHA (`Gov. Code § 12955`), Unruh (`Civ. Code § 51`), Federal FHA (`42 U.S.C. § 3604`), or SF `§ 37.10B(a)(9)`. Never raise an ineligible unit solely for appearance; document the neutral schedule for both units in writing.
