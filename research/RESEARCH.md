# San Francisco rent-increase research

**Research cutoff:** November 6, 2026. **Scope:** official-source legal and market-context research for a reported two-unit, 1940-built property in ZIP 94122. No address, APN, lease document, rent ledger, ownership record, subsidy agreement, or Rent Board decision was available. Reported private facts are not verified public facts.

## Executive conclusions

1. **Ordinary local allowance:** for a confirmed rent-controlled continuing tenancy, the ordinary annual percentage is **1.6% of verified base rent** for an increase effective March 1, 2026–February 28, 2027. Coverage, the rent-increase anniversary, prior increases, available banking, licensing, notice, and other tenant protections remain separate conditions. [SF Rent Board announcement](https://www.sf.gov/news--annual-rent-increase-3126-22827-announced); [Administrative Code § 37.3](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/san_francisco/latest/sf_admin/0-0-0-70881).
2. **No unit-specific authorization:** a reported 1940 construction year and two-unit count do not prove rent-control coverage. Current lawful base rent, exemptions, inventory compliance, and rent history must be verified. [Coverage guidance](https://www.sf.gov/learn-about-san-francisco-rental-laws); [Housing Inventory](https://www.sf.gov/rent-board-housing-inventory).
3. **State cap is not an alternative:** the published 8.8% maximum for August 1, 2026–July 31, 2027 applies to state-covered units, not as an elective replacement for a more protective local limit. [SF AB 1482 guidance](https://www.sf.gov/reports--california-tenant-protection-act-2019-ab-1482); [Civil Code § 1947.12](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1947.12.).
4. **Market rent is not a lawful increase reason by itself:** a long-standing tenant's below-market rent is expressly insufficient, without extraordinary circumstances, for a special-circumstances increase. [Rent Board Topic 323](https://www.sf.gov/information/topic-no-323-special-circumstances-petitions-based-rents-comparable-units).
5. **One unit versus both:** neither choice is a discrimination safe harbor. Evaluate actual reasons, comparable treatment, timing, protected-class effects, and retaliation—not merely equal percentage increases. [California Civil Rights Department](https://calcivilrights.ca.gov/housing/); [24 CFR § 100.65](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/cfr/24/100?link-type=pdf&sectionnum=65&year=mostrecent).
6. **Independent review is pending:** the dashboard applies a tenant-side challenge checklist but has not been reviewed or endorsed by a tenant lawyer, advocate, counselor, or agency. [Official referral directory](https://www.sf.gov/information/rent-board-referral-listing).

## 2. Local annual increases, banking, and exceptional routes

### Ordinary annual increase

Section 37.3(a)(1) licenses an annual increase of no more than 60% of the published CPI increase, never above 7%, for a landlord meeting the reporting requirement. The Rent Board's effective-date schedule supplies **1.6%** for March 1, 2026–February 28, 2027. Base rent excludes the temporary passthrough categories identified by the ordinance and agency guidance. Do not round the increase up. [§ 37.3](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/san_francisco/latest/sf_admin/0-0-0-70881); [rate announcement](https://www.sf.gov/news--annual-rent-increase-3126-22827-announced); [increase guidance](https://www.sf.gov/learn-about-rent-increases-san-francisco).

### Banking

Unused annual percentages may be banked subject to the ordinance and licensing. Rule 4.12 uses addition of percentages, not compounding or rounding up, and permits a rent increase only once in a twelve-month period. It calls for identifying the banked portion and the dates from which it derives. No cumulative banking cap appears in the inspected § 37.3(a)(2), Rule 4.12, or recent amendment entries; an unverified proposal is not treated as enacted law. **No banked amount is recommended because the complete history is missing.** [§ 37.3](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/san_francisco/latest/sf_admin/0-0-0-70881); [Rule 4.12](https://www.sf.gov/reports/february-2024/rent-board-rules-and-regulations-part-iv-rent-increases-not-requiring); [amendment list](https://media.api.sf.gov/documents/601_List_of_Ord_Amendments_11-2-26.pdf).

**Source discrepancy:** the banking summary's “24 full months” wording does not match Rule 4.12's twelve-month frequency language. This project follows the primary rule and flags the discrepancy rather than silently harmonizing it. Rule 4.12 also says that omission of its banking disclosure alone does not void an increase; that narrow rule does not excuse the ordinance's other mandatory notice requirements. [Banking summary](https://www.sf.gov/information--banked-rent-increases); [Rule 4.12](https://www.sf.gov/reports/february-2024/rent-board-rules-and-regulations-part-iv-rent-increases-not-requiring); [§ 37.3(b)](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/san_francisco/latest/sf_admin/0-0-0-70881).

## 5. Reasons and tenant-side scrutiny

A defensible explanation identifies the legal route, verified base rent, calculation, effective date, notice/service, and supporting records. “The market went up,” “the tenant has been here a long time,” “a new roommate moved in,” or “the building was sold” is not a stand-alone authorization for an unrestricted increase. [Topic 323](https://www.sf.gov/information/topic-no-323-special-circumstances-petitions-based-rents-comparable-units); [§ 37.3](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/san_francisco/latest/sf_admin/0-0-0-70881); [banking guidance](https://www.sf.gov/information--banked-rent-increases).

The tenant-side review asks:

- Is the unit actually covered, and is the asserted base rent supported by the complete ledger and lawful notices?
- Was the last ordinary increase at least twelve months earlier, and is the anniversary correct?
- Is any banked amount traceable to unused annual percentages, with no compounding or rounding up?
- Is the license in substantial compliance, and are required inventory filings current?
- Does the notice contain the required breakdown and use legally sufficient service and lead time?
- Is an asserted expense or capital route supported by a filed petition, certification, or prescribed worksheet where required?
- Did the increase follow a repair request, organizing activity, complaint, or other protected conduct?
- Are one-unit and two-unit decisions explained by lawful unit-specific facts rather than protected characteristics or inconsistent pretexts?
- Did a partial occupancy change involve an original occupant who remains in lawful possession?
- Are pricing tools and data sources compliant with SF and state restrictions?

Sources: [§ 37.3](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/san_francisco/latest/sf_admin/0-0-0-70881); [Rules 4.10/4.12](https://www.sf.gov/reports/february-2024/rent-board-rules-and-regulations-part-iv-rent-increases-not-requiring); [licensing](https://www.sf.gov/information--sec-3715-reporting-obligations-licensing); [Civil Code § 827](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=827.); [petition procedures](https://www.sf.gov/landlord-petitions-and-passthroughs); [retaliation statute](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1942.5.); [California fair housing](https://calcivilrights.ca.gov/housing/); [algorithmic-device rule](https://www.sf.gov/information/sec-3710c-use-and-sale-algorithmic-devices-prohibited).

Raising one unit can be lawful when the units have different verified bases, anniversaries, histories, or approved adjustments. Raising both does not cure discrimination or retaliation. Conversely, different dollar amounts are not automatically discriminatory. The relevant inquiry is the actual legal and factual basis, consistently applied. [24 CFR § 100.65](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/cfr/24/100?link-type=pdf&sectionnum=65&year=mostrecent); [California Civil Rights Department](https://calcivilrights.ca.gov/housing/).
