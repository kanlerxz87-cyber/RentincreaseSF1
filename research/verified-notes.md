## L1. Coverage — Rent Ordinance applies to units built on/before June 13, 1979
- Source: City & County of SF, Rent Board, "Learn about San Francisco rental laws"
  https://www.sf.gov/learn-about-san-francisco-rental-laws/
- Quote: "If your unit was built before June 14, 1979, and is not used as a single family dwelling, it may have both: Rent control; Eviction protection."
- Also lists units with eviction protection only: built after June 13, 1979; most single-family homes and condos (if moved in on/after Jan 1, 1996); substantial rehabilitation; units regulated by another government agency.

## L4. Mechanics: once per 12 months, base rent only, no rounding up
- Source: https://www.sf.gov/learn-about-rent-increases-san-francisco
- "Under the San Francisco Rent Ordinance, a landlord can only increase a tenant's rent by a certain percentage once every 12 months."
- "There is no limit on how much rent a landlord may first charge a tenant when renting an empty unit that is covered by rent control."
- "Landlords must calculate the rent increase on the tenant's base rent only. This includes any housing services like parking or storage."
- "Rent increases cannot be rounded up to the nearest dollar."
- "A landlord can impose the first annual increase 12 months after the date the tenant's lease began."

## L5. Additional occupants cannot trigger a rent increase
- Source: https://www.sf.gov/learn-about-rent-increases-san-francisco
- Quote: "A landlord cannot charge additional rent simply because a new (or additional) roommate has moved into a unit with the existing tenant. This is an unlawful rent increase, even if the lease or rental agreement provides for the additional charge."

## L6. Rent increase license required (Housing Inventory)
- Source: https://www.sf.gov/learn-about-rent-increases-san-francisco (section "Get a rent increase license")
- "A landlord must get a rent increase license before imposing annual allowable and/or banked rent increases on a tenant. To get a rent increase license, a landlord must first report into the Rent Board Housing Inventory."
- "This is effective July 1, 2022 (or March 1, 2023 for condominiums and buildings with 1-9 residential units)."

## L7. Notice: 30 days, or 90 days if increase > 10%
- Source: https://www.sf.gov/learn-about-rent-increases-san-francisco ("Giving notice")
- "30-day written notice of the proposed rent increase"; "90-day written notice if the increase (either by itself or combined with another increase in the same year) is more than 10%"; "If the notice is mailed, the landlord must give the tenant 5 more days."
- Notice should include dollar amount, percentage, effective date; use the percentage in effect on the effective date, not the notice date.
- Same on https://www.sf.gov/information--banked-rent-increases ("Which part of the increase is banked").

## L8. Banked increases — Rule 4.12 (12-month test)
- Source: Rent Board Rules and Regulations Part IV, published by SF.gov
  https://www.sf.gov/reports--december-2022--rent-board-rules-and-regulations-part-iv-rent-increases-not-requiring
- Rule 4.12(a): "A landlord who refrains from imposing an annual rent increase, or any portion thereof, may accumulate said increase and impose that amount on or after the tenant's subsequent rent increase anniversary date; however, the rent may be increased only one time every twelve (12) months. This banked amount may only be given at the time of an annual increase. Only those increases which could have been imposed on, or subsequent to, April 1, 1982, may be accumulated. A full 12 months must have elapsed from the date that an annual rent increase, or a portion thereof, could have been imposed before this banking section becomes applicable. Banked increases shall not be compounded and shall not be rounded up..."
- Rule 4.12(b): landlord must inform tenant which portion is banked and the dates upon which it is based; "failure to include such information shall not render the increase null and void."
- Rule 4.10(b): non-conforming notice renders the entire increase null and void, unless it equals no more than allowable annual+banked amounts, with a good-faith 0.5% tolerance readjustment by an ALJ.

## L9. Banked increases — SF.gov consumer page (states 24 months)
- Source: https://www.sf.gov/information--banked-rent-increases
- "A skipped increase is only banked if at least 24 full months pass without any rent increase. If less than 24 months pass, the skipped increase is lost and cannot be imposed at a later date."
- Also: "Banking has only been allowed since April 1, 1982"; "There is no limit on how much can be banked"; "Banked increases do not expire during a tenancy"; "New owners may use banked increases from prior owners"; "Banked increases do not carry over to a new tenant"; "They cannot be compounded or prorated."
- **IRREGULARITY**: this 24-month statement conflicts with Rule 4.12's "A full 12 months must have elapsed," and with the Rent Board's own multi-year examples (which apply a 12-month test). Flagged.

## L10. Multi-year / banked calculation examples (official)
- Source: https://www.sf.gov/information--annual-rent-increase-multiple-years
- Increases are added, not compounded; no petition needed; no rounding up; 30 days notice (35 if mailed); base rent only.
- Example: moved in 9/1/2016, increase on 9/1/2020 = 8.2% (2.2 + 1.6 + 2.6 + 1.8).
- Source: https://www.sf.gov/learn-about-rent-increases-san-francisco
- Example "multiple years on anniversary date": moved in 6/1/2023; first increase 6/1/2026 = 4.7% (3.1% banked [1.7 + 1.4] + 1.6% annual).
- Example "multiple years with new anniversary date": a 1.4% increase available 6/1/2025 is LOST if increase imposed 5/1/2026 because 12 months had not elapsed.

## S1. AB 1482 / Civil Code 1947.12 — statewide cap, and local-control carve-out
- Source: California Legislative Information, Civil Code 1947.12
  https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1947.12
- (a)(1): "shall not, over the course of any 12-month period, increase the gross rental rate ... more than 5 percent plus the percentage change in the cost of living, or 10 percent, whichever is lower, of the lowest gross rental rate charged ... during the 12 months prior".
- (a)(2): no more than two increments in 12 months.
- (b): "For a new tenancy in which no tenant from the prior tenancy remains in lawful possession ... the owner may establish the initial rental rate not subject to subdivision (a)."
- (d)(3) exemption: "Housing subject to rent or price control through a public entity's valid exercise of its police power consistent with Chapter 2.7 ... that restricts annual increases in the rental rate to an amount less than that provided in subdivision (a)."
