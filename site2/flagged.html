F01HIGHBanking test: 12 months or 24 months?
Rule 4.12(a) requires only 'a full 12 months' to have elapsed from the date an increase could have been imposed. The sf.gov 'Banked rent increases' page says a skipped increase is banked only if 'at least 24 full months pass without any rent increase'. The Board's own worked examples on that page apply 12 months, and its 'irregular periods' page also applies a 12-month test. This decides whether Unit 2 is 13.1% ($412.65) or 11.7% ($368.55), and whether Unit 1 is 3.0% ($119.85) or 1.6% ($63.92). Recommended: rely on Rule 4.12 (13.1% / 3.0%) but get written confirmation from a Rent Board counselor before serving notice; if the priority is that the number cannot be challenged at all, use 11.7% for Unit 2 and 1.6% for Unit 1.

F17MEDIUM§37.3(b)(5) says a nonconforming notice makes the increase void — Rule 4.10(b) carves out an exception
These two provisions pull in different directions and the difference is worth understanding before it is used against you. §37.3(b)(5) says 'Any rent increase which does not conform with the provisions of this section shall be null and void.' Rule 4.10(b) repeats that, but adds a proviso: the increase is not void where 'the amount requested equals no more than the allowable annual and banked rent increase(s)'. Rule 4.12(b) goes further and says omitting the banked breakdown 'shall not render the increase null and void'. The practical reading is that a notice stating the correct total, but with an incomplete breakdown, is very likely still enforceable — but a tenant representative will raise §37.3(b)(5) first, and there is no reason to give them the opening. State the dollar amount, the percentage, the effective date, and each component with its dates.
To close it: Use a notice that states each component separately rather than only the total		S34 S06 S05
