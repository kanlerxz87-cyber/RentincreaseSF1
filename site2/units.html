The two units

Anonymised tenancy records, the year-by-year accumulation, and every dollar figure recomputed from the published percentages.

How the two ceilings arise

The units are next door to each other, but the ceilings differ by a factor of four. That is not a property of the flats: it is a property of the tenancies. Unit 1's tenancy began in 2025 and has had at most one anniversary. Unit 2's tenancy began in 2019 and appears never to have been increased, so six annual allowances have accumulated. Banked percentages are added to the current year and applied once to the current base rent. They are never compounded, and the result is never rounded up.

Where Unit 2's 11.5% comes from

Six annual allowances fell due on 1 June in each of 2020 to 2025. On the owner's account, Unit 2 has been at $3,150 throughout. Each unused allowance is banked at the percentage that was available in that year — not at today's rate. That assumption is the single biggest open question on this site: there is no rent ledger in the record, so it is flagged as F03.

The 12-month versus 24-month question

The 1.4% that fell due on 1 June 2025 is the contested one. Rule 4.12 requires only 12 months to have elapsed since it became available, and on 1 June 2026 a full year had passed — so under the Rule it is banked. The sf.gov summary page says a skipped increase is banked only after 24 full months, which would exclude it and bring the total to 11.7% ($368.55). Both are shown. The Rule is the binding text; the summary is the page the Rent Board publishes for owners. Confirming the answer with a counselor before service costs a phone call.
