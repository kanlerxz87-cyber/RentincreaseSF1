What an individual owner should actually do

  1. File the inventory and get the licence. This is the only step that is genuinely administrative, and it is the one most often skipped. It is free.
  2. Write the rule down once and apply it to both tenancies. A one-page rent-setting policy — "the annual allowable increase is imposed on each tenancy's anniversary date, on base rent, together with any banked prior-year allowances" — is the single most useful document you can produce if either tenant ever asks why. It also answers the discrimination question you raised: the reason the two units are treated differently is visible on the page and has nothing to do with who lives there.
  3. Keep the file. Licence status for the effective date, last rent and last increase date, the notice and proof of service, the form 571 sheet for each percentage used. Everything a reviewer would ask for fits in one folder.
  4. Do not reach for a reason. The lawful reason is the published percentage. Adding a market-rent or life-circumstances explanation does not help and can create a retaliation or harassment issue where none existed.
