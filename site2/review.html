The tenant-lawyer review test

The fourteen questions a tenant-side reviewer would run, the pass condition for each, and the four that cannot be answered from the record.

The honest version of "would a tenant lawyer approve it"

A tenant-side reviewer does not ask whether the increase was reasonable in a business sense. They ask a short list of mechanical questions, in roughly this order, because any single wrong answer is enough. Nine of the fourteen below can be answered "yes" from the facts you supplied. One is answered "yes, but only on one reading of a rule the Rent Board states two different ways". Four cannot be answered from the record at all, and each of those is a place where a challenge could succeed on paperwork rather than on principle.

How a challenge would actually arrive

Not through a lawsuit, and not usually through a lawyer. A tenant can file an unlawful-rent-increase petition at the Rent Board, free, with no time limit, and have it heard by an Administrative Law Judge in a process the Board runs on the express basis that no one needs an attorney. That is the forum where every item above gets tested.

Sources: S15 S16

On the free-legal-advice point

San Francisco's free legal representation for tenants — the Tenant Right to Counsel programme — is triggered by a notice to terminate tenancy or an eviction suit. The Board's own notice form states it must be attached to every notice to terminate tenancy. A rent-increase dispute is a different track: a free Rent Board petition. This matters practically, because it means the realistic risk is a staff-reviewed petition about arithmetic and paperwork, not an adversarial lawsuit.

Sources: S31 S15

The four unanswerable items, and how to close them

  1. Licence status — check portal.sfrb.org for each unit (F04).
  2. Whether Unit 2 has been flat since 2019 — produce the rent ledger (F03).
  3. Whether Unit 1 was increased on 1 February 2026 — check the notice file (F07).
  4. Whether either rent is entirely base rent — check the leases (F05).

Each is answerable from the owner's own records, without any research. Closing all four converts the review from "passes on the arithmetic" to "passes on everything".
