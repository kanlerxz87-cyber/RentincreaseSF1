Rate limits, 2019 to 2027

The published annual percentages, which year each tenancy uses, and the four rules that convert a percentage into a lawful increase.

1.6%

is the annual allowable increase for rent-controlled units for increases effective 1 March 2026 through 28 February 2027. It is set at 60% of the increase in the Bay Area Consumer Price Index and can never exceed 7% in a year.

The rules that turn a percentage into a lawful increase

RuleWhat it meansOfficial source
Once every 12 monthsThe first increase can be imposed 12 months after the lease began. After that, no more than one increase in any 12 months. An increase imposed late makes its effective date the new anniversary.S01 S11
Base rent onlyExclude temporary passthroughs and fluctuating charges. Parking and storage rent generally do count as base rent.S01 S12
Add, never compoundSum the percentages first, then apply the sum once to the current base rent. Applying each year to a progressively larger rent overstates the ceiling.S05 S12
Never round upCarry the cent-level result. Rounding $63.92 up to $64 is not permitted.S01 S12
Percentage on the effective dateUse the rate in force when the increase takes effect, not the rate when the notice is served.S01 S04
10% switches the notice period10% or less: 30 days. More than 10%: 90 days. Add 5 days if served by mail.S01 S23 S28

Why the state's AB 1482 number is not the answer

The state cap (5% plus CPI, hard maximum 10%) is the figure most commonly quoted for California, but it does not apply to these units. Civil Code §1947.12(d)(3) excludes housing already subject to local rent control that restricts increases to less than the state cap. San Francisco's 1.6% is lower, so the local cap controls.
