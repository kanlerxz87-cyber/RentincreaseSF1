Do you have to involve the Rent Board?

The short answer is yes to the licence and no to an application. Here is the whole sequence, in the order it has to happen.

In one paragraph

Confirm the units are covered. Report both units into the Rent Board Housing Inventory, which generates the rent-increase licence automatically — this is the step you read about, and it is compulsory even for a single individual owner. Fix each tenancy's anniversary date. Add the current annual percentage to any properly banked years, on base rent only, without compounding or rounding up. Pick the notice period from the size of the total. Serve a written notice that states four specific things. Keep the evidence. You never file the notice with the Rent Board and you never ask it to approve an annual or banked increase.

The first annual increase can be imposed 12 months after the lease began; after that, no more than one increase every 12 months. An increase imposed later than the anniversary makes the effective date the new anniversary. Unit 1's tenancy began 1 Feb 2025, so 1 February is the anniversary on the supplied facts. Unit 2's tenancy began 1 June 2019, so 1 June is the anniversary.

For an increase effective on or before 28 Feb 2027 the annual percentage is 1.6% (period 1 March 2026 – 28 February 2027). Use the percentage in effect on the effective date of the increase, not the date of service. Skip anything that is a temporary passthrough or a fluctuating charge. 5Decide the notice period: 30 days, or 90 days if the total increase exceeds 10%
Any increase over 10% — including a total built from banked years — requires at least 90 days' written notice under both state law (Civ. Code §827(b)(3)) and the Rent Board's own guidance. If served by mail, add 5 calendar days (CCP §1013). Unit 2's 13.1% crosses the 10% line; Unit 1's 1.6% or 3.0% does not.

6Serve a written notice that contains all four required elements
Dollar amount; percentage amount; effective date; and which portion is the annual increase and which is banked, with the dates the banked amount is based on (Rule 4.10(a)(1) and Rule 4.12(b)). Serve by personal delivery, or by mail under CCP §1013. Keep proof of service. Rule 4.10(b) makes a non-conforming notice void — but expressly preserves an increase that equals no more than the allowable annual and banked amounts, which is the case here if you stay at or under the ceiling.

A new or additional roommate is expressly not a basis for extra rent, even if the lease says otherwise. A long tenancy below market rent is expressly not an extraordinary circumstance. Retaliation within 180 days of a tenantability complaint is unlawful. Targeting one tenant because they complained can be bad-faith harassment.

Retain: the Housing Inventory submission confirmation and licence status for the effective date; the prior rent and the date of the last increase; the notice with proof of service; the rate sheet (form 571) for each percentage used; and a short written rent-setting policy applied to both units. If challenged, a tenant can file a no-fee petition with no time limit, and cannot be required to have a lawyer.

Where this most often goes wrong

  1. The licence. Not a filing for approval — but if it is missing on the effective date, Rule 13.14 voids the whole increase. It is also the one item on this page that cannot be checked from your lease paperwork.
  2. The 10% threshold. Banked years push Unit 2 over 10%, which turns a 30-day notice into a 90-day notice. A 30-day notice on a 13.1% increase is defective.
  3. Using the wrong percentage. Use the rate in effect on the effective date, not the date you hand over the notice.
  4. Giving a reason that is not lawful. Market rent, the length of the tenancy and the April 2026 occupant change in Unit 1 are all expressly unavailable as grounds.
