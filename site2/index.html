Rent Increase Review — Outer Sunset, San Francisco 94122

What a lawful, and defensible, rent increase looks like for two rent-controlled flats in a 1940 building in the Outer Sunset. Every figure below is traced to an official source fetched on 2026-11-07.

The question you led with

Do I, as an individual owner (not a corporation), have to notify the Rent Board and get a rent-increase licence before raising rent?

YES to the licence. NO filed application or notice to the Rent Board for a normal annual or banked increase.

You do not file anything with the Rent Board to raise rent on an annual or banked schedule, and you do not need the Board's approval. What you do need is the rent-increase licence, and it is not an application: it is generated automatically once you report the unit in the annual Housing Inventory. Owning as an individual rather than a corporation changes nothing — the obligation attaches to the unit, not to the size of the owner. If the licence is not in place on the effective date of the increase, the entire increase is void.

Seven points that answer it

StatusPointOfficial source
VerifiedThe licence obligation applies to the unit, not the owner's size or corporate status
Ordinance §37.15(a) applies to 'Owners of residential dwelling units subject to this Chapter 37'. There is no exemption for an individual, a sole owner, a small landlord, or a two-unit building. The Housing Inventory page states that 'All owners of residential property are required to report.'		S08 S09
VerifiedWhat the licence actually is: the automatic result of reporting into the Housing Inventory
You do not apply for a licence as a separate step. On submitting the annual Housing Inventory report for a tenant-occupied unit, 'a rent increase license will be automatically generated'. Reporting is free and done online through the Rent Board Portal.		S09
VerifiedConsequence of raising rent without a current licence
Rule 13.14: an increase that purports to be effective while the owner is unlicensed 'shall render the entire rent increase null and void'. The test is the increase's EFFECTIVE DATE, not the date the notice is served. For buildings with fewer than 10 units this applies to increases effective on or after 1 March 2023. The statute also suspends the licence during noncompliance and says the tenant need not pay the increase for the suspended months. The Ordinance says the same in §37.3(b)(5): 'Any rent increase which does not conform with the provisions of this section shall be null and void.'		S07 S08 S34
VerifiedWhat you do NOT have to do: no application, no approval, no filing of the notice for an annual or banked increase
Annual and banked increases under §37.3(a)(1)–(2) need no petition and no approval: 'Landlords are not required to file a petition with the City to impose these types of rent increases.' Only the six listed petition-based increases (capital improvement, operating and maintenance, special circumstances/comparables, Proposition I history, utility passthrough, §1.21) require a petition, and those must be filed BEFORE the notice is served.		S12 S13
VerifiedWho you must actually notify: the tenants, in writing — not the Rent Board
The notice duty runs to the tenant: written notice stating the dollar amount, the percentage, the effective date, and which portion (if any) is banked. The licence itself 'does not need to be included with your rent increase notice.'		S01 S04 S09
VerifiedTwo separate annual obligations that are easy to confuse with the licence
(1) Housing Inventory report — updated annually by 1 March. (2) Rent Board Fee — $59.00 per dwelling unit for the 2026-27 tax year, also due 1 March, with penalties of 5% per month to a 15% cap. Paying the fee does not satisfy the Inventory requirement.		S09 S19
VerifiedOne timing trap that voids an otherwise perfect increase
'Your rent increase notice will not be enforceable without having a rent increase license on the effective date of the notice.' Because the test is the effective date, a licence obtained after service but before the effective date is sufficient — and a licence that lapses before the effective date is not.		S09 S07

One conflict you must resolve before serving notice

The Rent Board's binding Rule 4.12 requires a skipped annual increase to be banked after 12 months. Its public summary page says 24 months. The Board's own worked examples apply 12. That single difference decides whether Unit 2 is 13.1% ($412.65) or 11.7% ($368.55), and whether Unit 1 is 3.0% ($119.85) or 1.6% ($63.92). Both ranges are published here rather than picking one silently. See F01.
