Raising the rent on two 1940 flats in the Outer Sunset — what the Rent Board requires, and what the numbers actually are

Both units were built in 1940, so both are covered by the San Francisco Rent Ordinance. That single fact decides almost every question here: local law sets the ceiling, the Rent Board publishes it once a year, and the Board's licence — not the notice — is the step that decides whether an increase can be enforced at all.

Start here — the Rent Board questionYour first question, answered before anything else, because it decides whether the rest of the arithmetic can be used at all.

Do you, as a single individual owner, need a Rent Board rent-increase licence before you tell the tenants about an increase? Yes.

San Francisco Administrative Code §37.15 requires owners of residential dwelling units subject to Chapter 37 to report their units to the Rent Board. The Board then issues a rent increase licence. The Rent Board's own guidance says "Property owners who report that a unit is tenant-occupied will receive a rent increase license that allows them to impose annual allowable and banked rent increases" and that a current licence must be on file "before any annual or banked rent increase can go into effect". Nothing in the ordinance or the guidance exempts an individual owner, a two-unit building, or a landlord who is not a corporation.

But the licence is not an application about your increase, and you do not send the Rent Board the notice. It is generated automatically when you file the annual Housing Inventory report online, it costs nothing, and it is renewed by filing again each year by 1 March. Banked increases "do not have to be approved by the Rent Board". Beyond that report, the Board's role is petition-driven: a landlord petition if you want to exceed the allowance, or a tenant petition if the increase is challenged.

So the sequence you read about is real, but narrower than it sounds: file the annual Housing Inventory report → receive the licence → calculate the lawful amount → serve a notice that contains every required element → keep the proof. Skip step 1 and a perfectly calculated increase is unenforceable for the months the licence was suspended. Full step-by-step answer, with the exact text and links →

One number to act on now. The current allowance of 1.6% applies only to increases effective between 1 March 2026 and 28 February 2027. If Unit 2's accumulated 13.1% is to be imposed at that rate with the 90 days' notice a >10% increase requires, the notice has to be served by 30 November 2026 (25 November 2026 if mailed). After that, the effective date falls into a rate period the Rent Board has not yet published.
