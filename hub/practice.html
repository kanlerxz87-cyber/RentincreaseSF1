Question 4 of 6 · owner practice

Big owners, small owners, same ordinance

Institutional landlords and a sole owner of two flats are held to identical rent-increase rules in San Francisco. Algorithmic rent-setting, the tool most associated with portfolio-scale pricing, is unlawful here for everyone.

Owner-neutral duties§37.10C pricing banNo official behaviour data

The honest answer about practiceOfficial sources state the rules; they do not measure behaviour.

The question “how do big corporations handle increases, and how do individual owners?” has a verified half and an unverified half. The verified half is that the same ordinance applies to both — the same allowance, licence, notice period and banking rules — and that San Francisco prohibits algorithmic rent-setting that draws on non-public competitor data, for every landlord, large or small. That prohibition is the clearest official statement about how rents may be set at scale in this city. The unverified half is what typical owners actually do — no official dataset measures it, so nothing is asserted here. See flag H-11.

What this means for you as an individual owner

You are held to exactly the standard a large owner is held to, and you have none of their administrative slack. That cuts both ways: the compliance burden is small and fixed (one annual report, one notice, one calculation), and a small owner who does those three things properly is, on the documents, indistinguishable from a compliant institution.

What large operators do differently, where it is verifiedTwo citable differences, one of them a prohibition.

  • Pricing software is banned in San Francisco. §37.10C makes it unlawful to use an algorithmic device that performs calculations on non-public competitor data to advise a landlord on rent or occupancy, and gives tenants and tenant nonprofits a private right of action. It applies to every landlord, including you. S16
  • Corporate form closes a state-law exemption. The AB 1482 exemption for single-family homes and condominiums is unavailable to a REIT, a corporation, or an LLC with a corporate member. That exemption is irrelevant to these two units — both are already under the lower local cap — but it is the one place where owner type changes the analysis. S09
  • Everything else in the file is owner-neutral: the allowance, the licence, the notice period, the banking rules, the harassment and fair-housing exposure. S02 S01

The practical difference that does existNot legal, but worth naming.

Institutional owners run a compliance calendar; individual owners run the same rules from memory. The four dates that matter here are the annual 1 March Housing Inventory deadline, the anniversary date for each unit, the 90-day threshold for Unit 2's 13.1%, and the 28 February 2027 end of the 1.6% rate. Writing those four down is the entire difference between the two modes of practice.
