Big owners, small owners, same ordinance

Individual and institutional landlords share the core SF duties. A federal enforcement example illustrates one alleged portfolio-scale practice; San Francisco's covered algorithm ban applies to every landlord.

The honest answer about practiceBinding duties are verified; typical behavior by owner type is not.

The same allowance, license, notice, banking, harassment, and fair-housing rules apply to an individual and a corporate landlord. San Francisco also prohibits covered algorithmic devices using nonpublic competitor data. No official dataset measuring typical increase behavior for San Francisco owners by corporate form was located; the value remains blank at H-11.

What an individual owner should take from this

Use the same per-unit controls expected of any compliant operator: current reporting and license evidence, actual lease and ledger, reproducible calculation, neutral selection, timely notice, and proof of service. That is a recommendation, not a claim that small owners are inherently informal or large owners inherently compliant.

A documented corporate-practice exampleEnforcement allegations and a proposed settlement—not a universal stereotype or final adjudication.

  • DOJ alleged that RealPage's revenue-management software relied on landlords' nonpublic competitively sensitive information and included features aligning pricing. Its November 24, 2025 announcement described a proposed settlement subject to court approval. This is evidence about that enforcement matter, not about every corporation. S22
  • San Francisco's rule is direct: §37.10C prohibits a landlord from using a covered algorithmic device that analyzes nonpublic competitor data to recommend rents or occupancy. General property-management software is not banned merely because it is software. S16
  • Owner type can matter in a different state-law context: the AB 1482 single-family/condominium exemption has entity and notice conditions. It does not replace this building's lower local cap on the brief's rent-controlled premise. S09 S10
