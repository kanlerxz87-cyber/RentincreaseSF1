Occupant changes, §6.14 and Costa-HawkinsWhy Unit 1's April 2026 change does not reset the rent, and what still needs documenting.

Unit 1 was reported with three occupants at the February 2025 lease start and two on 1 April 2026, one of them a replacement. Under the official rules, a market-rate reset is keyed to the last original occupant leaving. On the reported facts one original occupant remained, so the 1.6% allowance still governs. Two private facts remain open and are flagged: whether a §6.14 notice was served under §6.14(b) for a co-occupant (H-23), and whether the incoming occupant is a co-tenant or a non-co-occupant subtenant governed by §6.14(c) and Costa-Hawkins (H-24). Preserving a future reset under §6.14Under Rent Board guidance and Rules §6.14(b), a 6.14 notice on a co-occupant must be served within a reasonable time after actual knowledge of the occupancy (60 days establishes a rebuttable presumption). For a non-co-occupant subtenant where the last original occupant vacates on or after April 25, 2000, Rules §6.14(c) turns on post-vacancy waiver—such as failing within 90 days of written notice or actual knowledge to serve an increase or reserve rights (§6.14(d) covers pre-April 25, 2000 departures).The reported change was 1 April 2026, so the sixty-day benchmark fell around 31 May 2026, before this review. Whether a notice was served, and whether any presumption can be rebutted, is a private fact (H-23).S27 S28
§6.14 is not the only routeWhere an occupant is a post-1996 lawful subtenant or assignee (not a co-tenant), Civil Code §1954.53(d) and Rules §6.14(c)/(d)(2) permit establishing a new base rent when the last original occupant no longer permanently resides, even without an initial 6.14 notice, unless waived.A missed or late 6.14 notice does not by itself end the analysis. Costa-Hawkins has its own limits, including that it does not reach a co-tenant who moved in after the original occupant (H-24).S28 S27
Co-tenant or subtenant?A co-tenant is not subject to a Costa-Hawkins increase regardless of when the co-tenant moved in. A co-tenant is someone with an oral or written agreement with the landlord, or whom the landlord has treated as a tenant by the acceptance of rent or other conduct. A 6.14 notice, by contrast, can be served on either a subtenant or a co-tenant.If the incoming Unit 1 occupant was added to the lease, or the owner has accepted rent directly from them, they may be a co-tenant. That status is a private fact and changes which route, if any, is available later (H-24).S27 S29
No pre-filing is required for either routeA landlord is not required to file a petition for approval of a §6.14 or Costa-Hawkins increase, but may file for a determination; a tenant may file a petition alleging an unlawful rent increase.If either route is ever relied on, the optional determination petition is a way to test it before serving.
