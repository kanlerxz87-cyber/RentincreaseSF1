Question 2 of 6 · limits and amounts

What a lawful increase is, to the cent, for both units

1.6% is the allowance for increases effective between 1 March 2026 and 28 February 2027. On these two rents that is $63.92 and $50.40 a month. Unit 2's six unused anniversaries add another 11.5%, which is lawful, additive, and the number most likely to be questioned.

What “reasonable and normal” means in lawThe only defensible benchmark is the published allowance. There is no official “market” figure for a covered unit.

For a sitting tenant in a covered unit, the ordinance is a price-control scheme, not a fairness standard. The lawful increase is the published percentage, plus anything properly banked, and nothing more without a petition. That is also the number a tenant lawyer will compare against, which is why it is the only figure worth planning around.

Unverified on purpose: no official source publishes a “normal” increase for 94122, and commercial listing data is not an official source, so no market figure appears anywhere on this site. The value is blank and flagged H-10.

Why the extra 1.4% is conditional. The Rent Board's banking page says a skipped increase is banked only if at least 24 full months pass without any increase; Rule 4.12(a) and the Board's worked examples use a 12-month test. 1 February 2027 is exactly 24 months after the last rent took effect, so it satisfies both readings — but confirm before including the extra $55.93. Banking rules, from the two official pages: a skipped increase can be saved and imposed later; banked increases do not expire during a tenancy; there is no limit on the total that can be banked; they cannot be compounded or prorated; they do not carry over to a new tenant; and they do not need the Board's approval.
