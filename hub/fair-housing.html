Question 5 of 6 · one unit or both

Is it safe to raise one rent and not the other?

Not automatically. Neutral documented differences are distinct from protected-class selection, retaliation, or bad-faith pressure. Both units must independently satisfy every rent-increase requirement.

FEHAFair Housing Act§37.10B(a)(9)Documented reasons

One unit or both?Different treatment is not automatically discrimination; identical treatment is not immunity.

Raising one unit and not the other is not, by itself, a fair-housing violation. The reason, evidence, and independent lawfulness of each increase control.

Chapter 37 and the cited fair-housing statutes do not require parallel increases. They prohibit discrimination or harassment because of protected characteristics, and state/local law separately addresses retaliation and bad-faith conduct. Use verified lease terms, current base rent, anniversary, and banked history—not assumptions or protected traits.

This is a reading of cited law, not a prediction or legal clearance. Unit 2's current base and both full histories remain unverified.

A neutral, reviewable policyUse the same method, not necessarily the same percentage.

  1. Write the method once: review each unit annually against its actual lease, verified base, last increase, published rate, and proven banked components. S05
  2. Keep a per-unit calculation and decision record, including a decision not to raise an ineligible unit.
  3. Do not add rent solely because a roommate joined. Section 37.3(a)(11) prohibits that surcharge; a separate cost petition is a different, unproven route. S02
  4. Review protected activity and repairs. A 180-day event is a trigger for §1942.5 analysis, not an automatic ban; repair petitions can independently defer increases. S12 S23
  5. Do not use covered pricing algorithms that analyze nonpublic competitor data. S16

Why “raise both to be safe” is not a safe harbourUniformity cannot cure retaliation, protected-class motive, bad arithmetic, or an early fixed-term increase.

A neutral method and complete records reduce avoidable ambiguity; they do not guarantee acceptance by a tenant, advocate, Rent Board judge, or court. Do not raise an ineligible unit merely to make the building look symmetrical.
