Question 5 of 6 · one unit or both

Is it safe to raise one rent and not the other?

Yes, if the difference is explained by the arithmetic and the record. Fair-housing law prohibits discrimination because of a protected characteristic, retaliation and bad-faith pressure to vacate — not differences in rent between two tenancies.

FEHAFair Housing Act§37.10B(a)(9)Documented reasons

One unit or both?The question is not what you do to the second unit; it is why the two units differ.

Raising one unit's rent and not the other is lawful. Raising either in a way that tracks a protected characteristic, or in bad faith, is not.

Nothing in Chapter 37, Civil Code §1947.12, Government Code §12955 or the federal Fair Housing Act requires an owner to treat two tenancies identically. What the statutes prohibit is discrimination or harassment because of a protected characteristic, retaliation, and bad-faith pressure to vacate. The two units here differ for objective reasons that are already in the file: base rents of $3,995.00 and $3,150.00, different anniversary dates, and seven years of accumulated allowances on one side and one on the other.

This is a reading of the cited statutory text, not legal advice, and not a prediction. Confirm with counsel or a Rent Board counselor if either tenancy involves a protected characteristic, a complaint, or a pending dispute.

What to do so that the difference is provably objectiveFive records, none of them burdensome.

  1. Write the policy once. “Each unit's rent is reviewed annually against the Rent Board allowance; differences reflect base rent, anniversary date and banked history.” Apply it to both units, including the unit you choose not to raise.
  2. Keep a per-unit calculation sheet showing base rent, percentage, dollar amount and effective date — the tables on Limits & amounts are that sheet.
  3. Never tie an increase to occupancy. The April 2026 change in Unit 1 cannot support a charge of any kind, even if the lease says it can. S02
  4. Keep the 180-day retaliation window clear of habitability complaints, inspections or disputes. S12
  5. Never use pricing software that draws on non-public competitor data. S16

Why “raise both to be safe” is not a safe harbourThere is no official rule that says parallel treatment cures a risk.

Treating both units the same does not make a difference disappear if the reason for an increase was unlawful, and treating them differently is not itself a violation. What an investigator or a tenant lawyer asks is: what is the documented reason, and does it stand up? Here it does — but only because each unit has its own arithmetic.

If you prefer a visible, uniform gesture, the lawful version is to apply the same method (annual review against the published allowance, same notice template, same documentation) rather than the same percentage, which the law does not require and the banked history does not support.
