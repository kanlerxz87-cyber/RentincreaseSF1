Question 1 of 6 · the rent increase licence

Do you have to apply to the Rent Board before raising the rent?

You need the licence — but not the application you were probably imagining. Here is the exact duty, the exact mechanism, and the exact consequence of skipping it, for a sole owner of a two-unit building in 94122.

§37.15§37.3Housing InventoryFree licenceRequired on the effective date

The short answer§37.15 and the Rent Board's Housing Inventory guidance, read together.

Yes — a sole individual owner of a two-unit building needs the rent increase licence. No — the Rent Board does not approve, license or receive the individual increase.

The licence requirement is drafted by reference to the unit, not the owner's size or corporate form: “Owners of residential dwelling units subject to this Chapter 37 shall be required to report certain information about their units to the Rent Board”. The Board's guidance repeats that all owners must report, that reporting began for buildings with fewer than 10 units on 1 March 2023, and that a tenant-occupied unit reported in the Housing Inventory receives a licence.

What you read is therefore half right in a way that matters. The licence is the anti-challenge step — an increase without it cannot be enforced. But it is not an application, and there is no separate “inform the Rent Board of my increase” filing for an annual or banked increase. You earn the licence once a year by reporting, and you spend it by serving a conforming notice.

Two prerequisites can void an otherwise perfect increase: (1) no licence on the effective date, and (2) a notice that does not conform. Both are cheap to fix before service and expensive to fix after it. Neither is affected by the owner being an individual rather than a corporation.

#QuestionAnswer, from the official textStatusSource
Q1Does a single individual owner (no corporation) need a rent increase licence?Yes. The duty is written for "owners of residential dwelling units subject to this Chapter 37" and the Housing Inventory page states that all owners of residential property must report, including units in buildings with fewer than 10 units and condominiums. There is no small-owner or individual-owner exemption.VerifiedS01 S03
Q2Is the licence an application that the Rent Board approves?No. It is generated automatically when the owner reports the unit in the Housing Inventory with a tenant in occupancy: "Property owners who report that a unit is tenant-occupied will receive a rent increase license". Reporting is free and is done online in the Rent Board Portal. No approval, hearing or fee is involved.VerifiedS03
Q3Does the owner have to send the Rent Board the rent increase notice, or ask permission for the annual or banked increase?No. Annual and banked increases within the ordinance limits are imposed by the owner: banked increases "do not have to be approved by the Rent Board". The Board-facing duty is the reporting duty, and the annual report includes the base rent (in $250 increments) and occupancy. Board involvement is required only for an increase above the allowance, which needs a landlord petition, and for any tenant challenge.VerifiedS04 S02 S01
Q4When must the licence exist?A current licence must be on file before any annual or banked increase "can go into effect". It does not have to be attached to the notice, but it must be live on the effective date. The reporting deadline is 1 March every year; for a building with fewer than 10 units, reporting began on 1 March 2023.VerifiedS03 S01
Q5What happens if the unit was never reported?The licence is "temporarily suspended" while the owner is not in compliance; it is restored prospectively once the information is filed, but the tenant is not obligated to pay the increased rent for the months during the suspension. That is the precise mechanism that makes an otherwise lawful increase challengeable — and it is cured by filing, not by anything in the notice.VerifiedS01
Q6Is the licence the same thing as the annual Rent Board fee?No. The licence is free and comes from the Housing Inventory report. The fee is separate: $59.00 per dwelling unit for the 2026-2027 tax year, due 1 March, of which up to $29.50 may be recovered from tenants. The Rent Board states expressly that requesting a fee exemption does not satisfy the Housing Inventory requirement.VerifiedS17 S03
Q7Can a tenant check whether the owner holds a licence?Yes. The Rent Board states that a tenant can search the Rent Board Portal to see whether the landlord is up to date. The Housing Inventory information reported by owners is public.VerifiedS03

Where this leaves these two unitsThe licence question is answerable today; the licence status is not, and that is the one thing to check first.

  • Both units need a current licence on the effective date. The reporting obligation started on 1 March 2023 for a building of this size, so it is not new.
  • Filing is free and takes one annual report. The $59.00 per-unit fee is separate and does not create or replace the licence.
  • The licence is not consent to a particular increase. The arithmetic and the notice still have to be right — see Limits & amounts and Notice & reasons.
  • Status is unverified here. The Rent Board Portal holds the record and tenants can search it, so assume it will be checked. See flag H-04.
