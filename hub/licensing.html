Question 1 of 6 · the rent increase licence

Do you have to apply to the Rent Board before raising the rent?

You need the licence — but not the application you were probably imagining. Here is the exact duty, the exact mechanism, and the exact consequence of skipping it, for an individual owner with two reported rent-controlled units in 94122.

§37.15§37.3Housing InventoryFree licenceRequired on the effective date

The short answer§37.15 and current Housing Inventory guidance, read together.

Yes — an individual owner needs a current license for each tenant-occupied unit. No — the Rent Board does not pre-approve each annual or banked increase.

The reporting duty applies to owners of units subject to Chapter 37, including buildings with fewer than 10 units. Corporate form is irrelevant. Once the owner substantially complies with reporting, a license is automatically generated for a non-owner-occupied unit. It is free and need not be attached to the tenant notice.

Before notice or before effectiveness? The official rule is that a current license must be on file when the annual/banked increase goes into effect. Completing the inventory and confirming the license before serving notice is the recommended risk-control sequence, not a claim that the statute always makes pre-notice licensure the hard cutoff. Late submission is possible, but an increase is unenforceable for the unlicensed period and restoration is prospective.

Ordinary annual/banked route: report/update inventory → confirm license → verify lease and ledger → calculate → serve the tenant with a conforming notice → confirm licensure on the effective date. Do not send an ordinary annual/banked notice to the Board as an approval request. Separate petition-based increases have different procedures.

Q1Does a single individual owner (no corporation) need a rent increase licence?Yes. The duty is written for "owners of residential dwelling units subject to this Chapter 37" and the Housing Inventory page states that all owners of residential property must report, including units in buildings with fewer than 10 units and condominiums. There is no small-owner or individual-owner exemption.VerifiedS01 S03
Q2Is the licence an application that the Rent Board approves?No. It is generated automatically when the owner reports the unit in the Housing Inventory with a tenant in occupancy: "Property owners who report that a unit is tenant-occupied will receive a rent increase license". Reporting is free and is done online in the Rent Board Portal. No approval, hearing or fee is involved.VerifiedS03
Q3Does the owner have to send the Rent Board the rent increase notice, or ask permission for the annual or banked increase?No for ordinary annual/banked increases: banked increases “do not have to be approved by the Rent Board,” and the tenant notice is not filed with the Board for approval. The Board-facing duty is Housing Inventory reporting. Certain increases above the annual/banked route require a landlord petition; general obligation bond and water revenue bond passthroughs use required worksheets instead of an approval petition, and certain utility passthroughs have their own worksheet/petition route.VerifiedS04 S02 S01 S31
Q4When must the licence exist?A current licence must be on file before any annual or banked increase "can go into effect". It does not have to be attached to the notice, but it must be live on the effective date. The reporting deadline is 1 March every year; for a building with fewer than 10 units, reporting began on 1 March 2023.VerifiedS03 S01
Q5What happens if the unit was never reported?The license is “temporarily suspended” while the owner is not in compliance. Filing restores it prospectively; it does not retroactively validate an increase that took effect while unlicensed. Rule 13.14 says an annual or banked increase imposed without a current license is null and void, while the underlying banking rights are preserved. Do not rely on a later filing to cure past unlicensed months.VerifiedS01 S30
Q6Is the licence the same thing as the annual Rent Board fee?No. The license is free and comes from the Housing Inventory report. The Rent Board Fee is separate: $59.00 per dwelling unit for the 2026–2027 tax year, due March 1 unless exempt. After full payment, up to 50% may be recoverable only from tenants in occupancy on the applicable November 1; penalties are not recoverable. Keep any recovery separate from base rent. Requesting a fee exemption does not satisfy Housing Inventory reporting.VerifiedS17 S34 S03
Q7Can a tenant check whether the owner holds a licence?Yes. The Rent Board states that a tenant can search the Rent Board Portal to see whether the landlord is up to date. The Housing Inventory information reported by owners is public.VerifiedS03
Q8Is the Rent Board licence the only licence an individual owner needs?There is a second, separate City registration that is often confused with it. The Business Registration Certificate is issued by the Office of the Treasurer & Tax Collector as a general business-tax registration. Do not treat one registration as satisfying the other. The Housing Inventory asks for a business registration number “if any”; the Rent Increase License still comes from substantial Housing Inventory compliance.VerifiedS24 S25 S03
Q9Does this owner need a Business Registration Certificate?Not established from the brief. If this is one residential structure with fewer than four units and rental income is the only business activity, Business and Tax Regulations Code §853(d) says that fact alone does not require a certificate. The brief supplies two rent-controlled units, not the total unit count in the structure or other business activity. Confirm those facts before relying on the exception; do not infer a two-unit building from two reported units.Needs evidenceS24 S25 S42
Q10When would a Business Registration Certificate become required?The exception is conditional. Other San Francisco business activity, payroll expense, or short-term residential rental income can trigger registration; the Tax Collector determines the actual unit count. The “Qualified Lessor” gross-receipts exclusion is a narrow nonprofit-managed limited-partnership definition, not a blanket individual-owner exemption. This is logged as H-21 and must be confirmed against the owner’s records and, if needed, the Tax Collector.Needs evidenceS24 S25 S42
