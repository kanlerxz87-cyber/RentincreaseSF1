Question 1 of 6 · the rent increase licence

Do you have to apply to the Rent Board before raising the rent?

You need the licence — but not the application you were probably imagining. Here is the exact duty, the exact mechanism, and the exact consequence of skipping it, for a sole owner of a two-unit building in 94122.

§37.15§37.3Housing InventoryFree licenceRequired on the effective date

The short answer§37.15 and current Housing Inventory guidance, read together.

Yes — an individual owner needs a current license for each tenant-occupied unit. No — the Rent Board does not pre-approve each annual or banked increase.

The reporting duty applies to owners of units subject to Chapter 37, including buildings with fewer than 10 units. Corporate form is irrelevant. Once the owner substantially complies with reporting, a license is automatically generated for a non-owner-occupied unit. It is free and need not be attached to the tenant notice.

Before notice or before effectiveness? The official rule is that a current license must be on file when the annual/banked increase goes into effect. Completing the inventory and confirming the license before serving notice is the recommended risk-control sequence, not a claim that the statute always makes pre-notice licensure the hard cutoff. Late submission is possible, but an increase is unenforceable for the unlicensed period and restoration is prospective.

Ordinary annual/banked route: report/update inventory → confirm license → verify lease and ledger → calculate → serve the tenant with a conforming notice → confirm licensure on the effective date. Do not send an ordinary annual/banked notice to the Board as an approval request. Separate petition-based increases have different procedures.

The licence question, in the order an owner needs it answered.
#QuestionAnswer, from the official textStatusSource
Q1Does a single individual owner (no corporation) need a rent increase licence?Yes. The duty is written for "owners of residential dwelling units subject to this Chapter 37" and the Housing Inventory page states that all owners of residential property must report, including units in buildings with fewer than 10 units and condominiums. There is no small-owner or individual-owner exemption.VerifiedS01 S03
Q2Is the licence an application that the Rent Board approves?No. It is generated automatically when the owner reports the unit in the Housing Inventory with a tenant in occupancy: "Property owners who report that a unit is tenant-occupied will receive a rent increase license". Reporting is free and is done online in the Rent Board Portal. No approval, hearing or fee is involved.VerifiedS03
Q3Does the owner have to send the Rent Board the rent increase notice, or ask permission for the annual or banked increase?No. Annual and banked increases within the ordinance limits are imposed by the owner: banked increases "do not have to be approved by the Rent Board". The Board-facing duty is the reporting duty, and the annual report includes the base rent (in $250 increments) and occupancy. Board involvement is required only for an increase above the allowance, which needs a landlord petition, and for any tenant challenge.VerifiedS04 S02 S01
Q4When must the licence exist?A current licence must be on file before any annual or banked increase "can go into effect". It does not have to be attached to the notice, but it must be live on the effective date. Adopted Rule §13.14 states the gate expressly: the effective date, not the date of service, determines whether the increase conforms. The reporting deadline is 1 March every year; for a building with fewer than 10 units, reporting began on 1 March 2023.VerifiedS03 S01 S30
Q5What happens if the unit was never reported?The licence is "temporarily suspended" while the owner is not in compliance; it is restored prospectively once the information is filed, but the tenant is not obligated to pay the increased rent for the months during the suspension. Rule §13.14 adds that an unlicensed increase is null and void yet "Nothing in this Regulation shall affect a landlord's banking rights or the ability to accumulate otherwise lawful rent increases that were found to be null and void." That is the precise mechanism that makes an otherwise lawful increase challengeable — and it is cured by filing, not by anything in the notice.VerifiedS01 S30
Q6Is the licence the same thing as the annual Rent Board fee?No. The licence is free and comes from the Housing Inventory report. The fee is separate: $59.00 per dwelling unit for the 2026-2027 tax year, due 1 March, of which up to $29.50 may be recovered from tenants. For the 2027 Rent Board fee (due 1 March 2027), the Rent Board states a December 14, 2026 deadline to submit a Request for Exemption. Requesting a fee exemption does not satisfy the Housing Inventory requirement.VerifiedS17 S03
Q7Can a tenant check whether the owner holds a licence?Yes. The Rent Board states that a tenant can search the Rent Board Portal to see whether the landlord is up to date, by address or Assessor's Parcel Number, and can check whether a licence was on file on the effective date. The Housing Inventory information reported by owners is public. If there was no licence, the tenant may file a Tenant Petition for Unlawful Rent Increase after the effective date, or a Tenant Summary Petition before it, and may be entitled to a rent refund for an unlicensed increase that took effect.VerifiedS03 S31
Q8Is the Rent Board licence the only licence an individual owner needs?No — there is a second, separate City registration that is often confused with it. The Business Registration Certificate is issued by the Office of the Treasurer & Tax Collector, not the Rent Board, and it is a general business-tax registration. It is unrelated to whether a rent increase is lawful, and holding or not holding it does not affect the rent increase licence.VerifiedS24 S25 S01
Q9Does this owner need a Business Registration Certificate?On the reported facts, probably not. The Tax Collector states that a person receiving rental income from "one residential structure consisting of fewer than four units" is "not, by reason of that fact alone, required to obtain a business registration certificate", and lists the same carve-out among its registration triggers. Two units in one building is fewer than four. The same page states that such a lessor need not file a Gross Receipts return unless they lease four or more units in an individual building.Reading of cited textS24 S25
Q10When would a Business Registration Certificate become required?The exemption is expressly conditional. The Tax Collector warns that "if the same person engages in other business activities or incurs payroll expense, a business registration certificate may be required", and short-term residential rental income requires registration "regardless of the amount". The determination also depends on the actual unit count in the building, which the Tax Collector is authorised to decide. This is logged as flag H-21 and must be confirmed against the owner's own facts and, if needed, the Tax Collector's Help Center.Needs evidenceS24 S25
