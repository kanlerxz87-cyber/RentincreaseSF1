S01Sec. 37.15 — Reporting Obligations; Licensing (S.F. Admin. Code ch. 37)
San Francisco Rent Board (SF.gov) · Local — enacted ordinance		“(a) Owners of residential dwelling units subject to this Chapter 37 shall be required to report certain information about their units to the Rent Board … (e) If a landlord has substantially complied with the obligation to report information about a rental unit as required under this Section 37.15, the landlord shall receive a license to impose rent increases on tenants in that unit under Sections 37.3(a)(1)-(2). If the landlord has not substantially complied with the reporting obligation, then the license to impose rent increases shall be temporarily suspended during the period of the landlord's noncompliance … but a tenant shall not be obligated to pay the increased rent for months during the period of suspension.”The rent increase licence: who must hold it, how it is obtained, and the consequence of not holding it.
S02Sec. 37.3 — Rent Limitations (S.F. Admin. Code ch. 37)
San Francisco Rent Board (SF.gov) · Local — enacted ordinance		“(a)(1) Annual Rent Increase. On March 1 of each year, the Board shall publish the increase in the CPI … A landlord who has reported the required information about their rental unit to the Rent Board as set forth in Section 37.15 shall have a license to impose annually a rent increase which does not exceed a tenant's base rent by more than 60% of said published increase. In no event, however, shall the allowable annual increase be greater than 7%. … (b)(5) Nonconforming Rent Increases. Any rent increase which does not conform with the provisions of this section shall be null and void. … (a)(11)(A) … a landlord may not impose increases solely because a tenant has added an additional occupant to an existing tenancy … notwithstanding a rental agreement or lease that specifically permits a rent increase for additional occupants.”The annual allowance, the 7% ceiling, the licence condition written into the ordinance, notice contents, the null-and-void rule, and the additional-occupant prohibition.
S03The Rent Board Housing Inventory — and the rent increase licence
San Francisco Rent Board (SF.gov) · Local — agency guidance		“All owners of residential property are required to report … Property owners who report that a unit is tenant-occupied will receive a rent increase license that allows them to impose annual allowable and banked rent increases. … Deadline: March 1 of every year. … a landlord must have a current rent increase license on file with the Rent Board before any annual or banked rent increase can go into effect. The rent increase license does not need to be included with your rent increase notice. … Reporting is free.”Step-by-step licence mechanics, the annual deadline, the fact that the licence is free and automatic, and that it need not be attached to the notice.
S04Banked rent increases
San Francisco Rent Board (SF.gov) · Local — agency guidance		“A landlord may raise the rent once every 12 months by the allowed annual increase. … A skipped increase is only banked if at least 24 full months pass without any rent increase. If less than 24 months pass, the skipped increase is lost and cannot be imposed at a later date. … Banked increases do not have to be approved by the Rent Board, but must be calculated correctly. They cannot be compounded or prorated. … There is no limit on how much can be banked.”Banking: accumulation, the 24-month statement that conflicts with Rule 4.12(a) (flag H-01), no Board approval, no compounding, and the notice contents for a banked increase.
S05Learn about rent increases in San Francisco
San Francisco Rent Board (SF.gov) · Local — agency guidance		“The allowed rent increase percentage in San Francisco is 1.6%. The percentage is effective March 1, 2026 through February 28, 2027. … A landlord can impose the first annual increase 12 months after the date the tenant's lease began. … 90-day written notice if the increase (either by itself or combined with another increase in the same year) is more than 10% … Rent increases cannot be rounded up to the nearest dollar. … A landlord cannot charge additional rent simply because a new (or additional) roommate has moved into a tenant's unit.”The current rate, the 12-month rule, notice periods, the no-rounding rule, the additional-occupant rule, and the worked examples used for the 12-month banking test.

S07Rent Board Rules and Regulations, Part IV — §§4.10 (Notice) and 4.12 (Banking)
San Francisco Rent Board (SF.gov) · Local — adopted regulations		“§4.12(a) … the rent may be increased only one time every twelve (12) months … A full 12 months must have elapsed from the date that an annual rent increase, or a portion thereof, could have been imposed before this banking section becomes applicable. Banked increases shall not be compounded and shall not be rounded up … §4.12(b) … failure to include such information shall not render the increase null and void. §4.10(b) Any rent increase which does not conform with the provisions of this Section shall render the entire rent increase null and void, unless the amount requested equals no more than the allowable annual and banked rent increase(s).”The 12-month banking test that conflicts with the 24-month wording on the Rent Board's banking page (flag H-01); the non-compounding and no-rounding rules; the itemisation duty and its exception (flag H-15).
S08California Civil Code §827 — notice of change in terms of tenancy
California Legislative Information · State — statute		“(b)(1) … the landlord may increase the rent … (A) By delivering a copy to the tenant personally. S09California Civil Code §1947.12 — Tenant Protection Act rent cap (AB 1482)
California Legislative Information · State — statute		“(a)(1) … an owner … shall not, over the course of any 12-month period, increase the gross rental rate … more than 5 percent plus the percentage change in the cost of living, or 10 percent, whichever is lower … (d) This section shall not apply to the following … (3) Housing subject to rent or price control through a public entity's valid exercise of its police power … that restricts annual increases in the rental rate to an amount less than that provided in subdivision (a).”Why the state cap does not apply to locally controlled units, and the statutory basis of the exemption.
