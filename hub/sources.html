S01Sec. 37.15 — Reporting Obligations; Licensing (S.F. Admin. Code ch. 37)
San Francisco Rent Board (SF.gov) · Local — enacted ordinance		“(a) Owners of residential dwelling units subject to this Chapter 37 shall be required to report certain information about their units to the Rent Board … (e) If a landlord has substantially complied with the obligation to report information about a rental unit as required under this Section 37.15, the landlord shall receive a license to impose rent increases on tenants in that unit under Sections 37.3(a)(1)-(2). If the landlord has not substantially complied with the reporting obligation, then the license to impose rent increases shall be temporarily suspended during the period of the landlord's noncompliance … but a tenant shall not be obligated to pay the increased rent for months during the period of suspension.”The rent increase licence: who must hold it, how it is obtained, and the consequence of not holding it.
S02Sec. 37.3 — Rent Limitations (S.F. Admin. Code ch. 37)
San Francisco Rent Board (SF.gov) · Local — enacted ordinance		“(a)(1) Annual Rent Increase. On March 1 of each year, the Board shall publish the increase in the CPI … A landlord who has reported the required information about their rental unit to the Rent Board as set forth in Section 37.15 shall have a license to impose annually a rent increase which does not exceed a tenant's base rent by more than 60% of said published increase. In no event, however, shall the allowable annual increase be greater than 7%. … (b)(5) Nonconforming Rent Increases. Any rent increase which does not conform with the provisions of this section shall be null and void. … (a)(11)(A) … a landlord may not impose increases solely because a tenant has added an additional occupant to an existing tenancy … notwithstanding a rental agreement or lease that specifically permits a rent increase for additional occupants.”The annual allowance, the 7% ceiling, the licence condition written into the ordinance, notice contents, the null-and-void rule, and the additional-occupant prohibition.
S03The Rent Board Housing Inventory — and the rent increase licence
San Francisco Rent Board (SF.gov) · Local — agency guidance		“All owners of residential property are required to report … Property owners who report that a unit is tenant-occupied will receive a rent increase license that allows them to impose annual allowable and banked rent increases. … Deadline: March 1 of every year. You can still make a Housing Inventory submission and receive a rent increase license after the deadline. However, your rent increase notice will not be enforceable without having a rent increase license on the effective date of the notice. … a landlord must have a current rent increase license on file with the Rent Board before any annual or banked rent increase can go into effect. S04Banked rent increases
San Francisco Rent Board (SF.gov) · Local — agency guidance		“Notice: Proposed Changes to Rent Increase Limitations. Posted November 9, 2026. The San Francisco Board of Supervisors is considering legislation (File No. 261020) that would temporarily limit the combined amount of banked rent increases and capital improvement rent increases that may be imposed on rent-controlled units to 10% per year. If enacted as currently proposed, the legislation would apply to banked rent increases noticed on or after September 15, 2026, and capital improvement rent increases based on petitions filed with the Rent Board on or after September 15, 2026. This legislation has not been enacted and is subject to change. --- A landlord may raise the rent once every 12 months by the allowed annual increase. … A skipped increase is only banked if at least 24 full months pass without any rent increase. If less than 24 months pass, the skipped increase is lost and cannot be imposed at a later date. … Banked increases do not have to be approved by the Rent Board, but must be calculated correctly. They cannot be compounded or prorated. … There is no limit on how much can be banked.”Banking: the 24-month no-increase statement, partial increases, no Board approval, no compounding, and notice contents. Read with Rule 4.12(a), which measures 12 months from when the skipped allowance became available (H-01). Now also carries the Rent Board's own File #261020 notice (H-22).

S27Rent Increases Under Section 6.14 and Costa-Hawkins
San Francisco Rent Board (SF.gov) · Local — Rent Board rule summary		“Rules and Regulations Section 6.14 provides for an unlimited rent increase on remaining occupants when the last original occupant vacates the unit, as long as the landlord timely served all remaining occupants with a written "6.14 notice." … The 6.14 notice must be served on each subsequent occupant within a reasonable time after the landlord knows or should have known of the occupancy. Generally, sixty days is considered reasonable. A 6.14 notice can be served on any subsequent occupant, whether the subsequent occupant is a subtenant or a co-tenant. … In addition, a co-tenant, as opposed to a subtenant, is not subject to such a rent increase, regardless of when the co-tenant moved in. A co-tenant is someone who has an oral or written agreement with the landlord, or who has become a tenant by the conduct of the parties such as the landlord's acceptance of rent.”What an occupant change does and does not allow: the §6.14 notice, the sixty-day reasonableness benchmark, and the co-tenant versus subtenant distinction.
S28Rent Board Rules and Regulations, Part VI — §6.14 Establishing Rental Rates for Subsequent Occupants
San Francisco Rent Board (SF.gov) · Local — Rent Board rules and regulations		“Failure to give such a notice within 60 days of the landlord's actual knowledge of the occupancy by the subsequent occupant(s) establishes a rebuttable presumption that notice was not given within a reasonable period of time; or (2) The landlord is entitled to establish a new base rent under the Costa Hawkins Rental Housing Act, California Civil Code Section 1954.53(d), even if no notice was served on the subsequent occupant(s) pursuant to subsection (d)(1) above.”The exact sixty-day rebuttable-presumption rule, and that Costa-Hawkins can apply even where no 6.14 notice was served.
S29Petition for a Determination Pursuant to Section 6.14 and/or Costa-Hawkins
San Francisco Rent Board (SF.gov) · Local — agency guidance		“A landlord is not required to file a petition with the Rent Board for approval of a rent increase pursuant to Section 6.14 or Costa-Hawkins Section 1954.53(d). However, if a landlord is unsure whether a rent increase is justified, the landlord may file a petition at the Rent Board to seek a determination that either or both sections authorize a rent increase. … Finally, Section 6.14 requires the last original occupant to "vacate" the unit, while Costa-Hawkins only requires the last original occupant to no longer "permanently reside" in the unit.”That no pre-filing is required, that an optional determination petition exists, and the vacate versus permanently-reside distinction.
