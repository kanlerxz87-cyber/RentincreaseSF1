Owner Practice

How institutional owners actually handle increases, with the individual-owner equivalent for each practice.

Short answer

Large owners do not have a different rule book. The evidence available from a public filer shows a professional operating model — one-year leases, deliberately staggered expirations, centralised renewal administration, data-driven pricing — layered on top of exactly the same statutory constraints that apply to a two-unit owner S23. What differs is administrative discipline, not legal permission. In San Francisco the binding number is the Rent Board's published percentage, and the most sophisticated tool in the corporate kit — algorithmic rent-setting — is expressly prohibited by the local ordinance S08.

What the filing actually says, and what it does not

“The construction, operation and leasing of our communities is subject to federal, state and local laws and regulations, include … fair housing laws, and, depending on the jurisdiction, regulations regarding the charging of rents and fees and increases in such amounts upon renewal of leases.”

“Some laws relating to the setting of rents apply broadly, such as in California, where residential rent increases at renewal in communities older than fifteen years are limited to the lesser of 10% or 5% plus local consumer price index (CPI) …” S23

Two things follow, and one important thing does not. First, a public company describes rent regulation as a material business constraint and puts renewal increases inside a compliance framework rather than treating them as a free commercial decision. Second, the description quotes the statewide cap: it does not describe San Francisco's stricter local cap, which is 1.6% here. A general filing is therefore not a source for a local number, and this report does not use it as one — the local number comes from the Rent Board S01 S03.

The practical takeaway. The defensible posture for a small owner is to mirror what a compliance department would do: one file per unit, one anniversary date, one written notice with the prescribed contents, one calculation sheet, and no explanation offered beyond the statutory basis — because the statutory basis is the only one that survives review S01 S08 S07.
