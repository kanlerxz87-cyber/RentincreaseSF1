That percentage is not permission to increase either reported unit: coverage, base rent, anniversary, prior increases, banking, license/inventory compliance, notice, and tenant protections remain separate checks. [SF rate announcement](https://www.sf.gov/news--annual-rent-increase-3126-22827-announced); [SF § 37.3](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/san_francisco/latest/sf_admin/0-0-0-70881); [licensing guidance](https://www.sf.gov/information--sec-3715-reporting-obligations-licensing).

## What makes a reason defensible

A defensible increase identifies the legal route, verified base, transparent calculation, correct timing, required filings, valid notice/service, and a legitimate non-retaliatory reason. “Market rent is higher,” “the tenant has stayed a long time,” “the building sold,” or “a roommate moved in” is not a stand-alone unrestricted increase authorization. A special-circumstances petition requires extraordinary circumstances beyond ordinary market conditions; a long-standing below-market tenancy alone is expressly insufficient. [Topic 323](https://www.sf.gov/information/topic-no-323-special-circumstances-petitions-based-rents-comparable-units); [SF § 37.3](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/san_francisco/latest/sf_admin/0-0-0-70881); [banking guidance](https://www.sf.gov/information--banked-rent-increases).

Banking must be reconstructed from unused annual percentages and license history. Rule 4.12 uses additive percentages, no compounding or rounding up, and a twelve-month ordinary-increase frequency rule. **No banked amount is recommended.** The agency summary's “24 full months” wording conflicts with the primary rule and is flagged. [Rule 4.12](https://www.sf.gov/reports/february-2024/rent-board-rules-and-regulations-part-iv-rent-increases-not-requiring); [banking summary](https://www.sf.gov/information--banked-rent-increases).

## One unit versus both

Raising one unit is not automatically discriminatory; raising both is not a safe harbor. Different amounts may reflect lawful differences in base rent, anniversary, history, or approved adjustments. The owner should document a neutral policy, unit-specific reasons, exceptions, and the decision timeline. A tenant-side reviewer should test protected-class treatment, familial-status/occupancy issues, and retaliation after repairs, complaints, or organizing. The 180-day retaliation provision is conditional, not a universal safe harbor after expiration. [California Civil Rights Department](https://calcivilrights.ca.gov/housing/); [24 CFR § 100.65](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/cfr/24/100?link-type=pdf&sectionnum=65&year=mostrecent); [Civil Code § 1942.5](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1942.5.).
