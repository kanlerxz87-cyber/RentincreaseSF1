Raising one unit vs. both — discrimination analysis

You are NOT required to raise both units by the same amount or to raise both units at all. But differences between units must rest on neutral, documented, non-protected reasons (anniversary date, base rent, banked history, lease start date, capital-improvement petitions). A difference motivated by a protected characteristic — race, national origin, religion, sex, gender identity/expression, sexual orientation, familial status, disability, marital status, source of income, age, immigration status — or by retaliation within 180 days of a repair complaint or agency contact, is unlawful under FEHA, the SF Rent Ordinance, and the federal Fair Housing Act.

Sources: [S10] California Government Code § 12955 — Fair Employment and Housing Act (FEHA, discrimination); [S11] 42 U.S.C. § 3604 — Federal Fair Housing Act

Practical recommendation

Applying the published annual rate on each unit's anniversary date, with banked amounts applied in a transparent, year-by-year schedule, is the most defensible uniform practice. It produces lawful differences in dollar amount between units because base rents and banked years differ. Apply and document the same policy to both units; do not vary treatment based on who the tenants are, their household composition, or whether they asserted rights.

Sources: [S10] California Government Code § 12955 — Fair Employment and Housing Act (FEHA, discrimination); [S11] 42 U.S.C. § 3604 — Federal Fair Housing Act

Key rules

  • Federal Fair Housing Act (42 U.S.C. §3604) and California FEHA (Gov. Code §12955) prohibit discrimination in the terms, conditions, or privileges of rental — which includes rent increases.
  • SF adds additional protected classes (source of income, immigration status, gender identity/expression, etc.).
  • Retaliation within 180 days of protected tenant activity (repair requests, Rent Board petitions, inspections, organizing) creates a presumption against the landlord.
  • Treating units differently is allowed when the difference is explained by neutral, documented factors (anniversary date, lease start date, banked history, approved passthroughs).
