Step-by-step process

Follow this order. The sequence — file → license → calculate → notice → serve → record — is the part that most often trips up small owners.

  1. Step 1: File / update the Rent Board Housing Inventory for both units
    Log in to the Rent Board Portal (https://portal.sfrb.org/) and confirm both units are reported as non-owner-occupied (tenant-occupied), with the required fields (bedrooms/bathrooms, approximate sq ft, occupancy date, base rent in $250 increments, utilities, owner/manager contact). Do this BEFORE the increase effective date. Annual deadline is March 1; late filing is accepted but the license only exists from the point of compliance.
    Sources: [S02] SF.gov — The Rent Board Housing Inventory (FAQ); [S03] SF.gov — New Legislation Creates A Housing Inventory And Requires Owners To Obtain a License Before Imposing Annual/Banked Rent Increases (Ordinance 265-20, eff. 1/18/2021); [S12] SF Rent Board Portal (Housing Inventory / License lookup / fee payment)
  2. Step 2: Confirm the rent-increase license is active for each unit
    In the Rent Board Portal, verify the license status for both units shows active for the date the increase will take effect. Tenants can also search this portal, so expect them to verify.
    Sources: [S02] SF.gov — The Rent Board Housing Inventory (FAQ); [S12] SF Rent Board Portal (Housing Inventory / License lookup / fee payment)
  3. Step 3: Pay the annual Rent Board fee ($59 per dwelling unit for 2026–27)
    Pay through the portal by March 1. This is separate from the license. Up to 50% may be recoverable from tenants as a fee passthrough (not as rent), using the Board's passthrough procedure.
    Sources: [S13] SF.gov — Rent Board Fee information
  4. Step 4: Calculate the increase using the published percentage in effect on the effective date (NOT the date you serve notice)
    Through 2/28/2027 the rate is 1.6%. Multiply base rent × 0.016. Do NOT round up. Add banked amounts from prior eligible years that were never used (added, never compounded). A new rate will be published for effective dates on/after 3/1/2027.
    Sources: [S01] SF.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco; [S04] SF.gov — Annual Rent Increase for 3/1/26 – 2/28/27 Announced (1.6%); [S05] SF.gov — Current Rates, including Rent Increase, Relocation, Sec. Deposit; [S06] SF.gov — Form 571 Allowable Annual Increases (2026–2027, English); [S14] SF Rent Board Rules and Regulations, Part 4 — Annual Increases (banking Rule 4.12)
  5. Step 5: Determine the notice period
    30 days' written notice for an increase of 10% or less; 90 days' written notice if the increase (alone or combined with other increases in the prior 12 months) exceeds 10%. Add 5 calendar days if served by mail within California (CCP §1013).
    Sources: [S01] SF.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco; [S07] California Civil Code § 827 — Notice of rent increase (state notice periods); [S08] California Code of Civil Procedure § 1013 — Service by mail (+5 days in-state)
  6. Step 6: Prepare a written notice that states the dollar amount, percentage, effective date, and itemises any banked amounts by year
    The SF Rent Board guidance says the notice should state the dollar amount, the percentage, and the effective date. Best practice (and the safer choice under tenant-lawyer scrutiny) is to also itemise which portion is the current annual allowance and which portion is banked, and to identify each banked year. Failure to itemise banking is a common tenant-petition ground even though Rule 4.10(b)/4.12(b) say omission does not automatically void an otherwise-within-cap increase.
    Sources: [S01] SF.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco; [S14] SF Rent Board Rules and Regulations, Part 4 — Annual Increases (banking Rule 4.12)
  7. Step 7: Serve the notice (personal delivery or mail)
    Personal delivery OR mail per CCP §1013 (add 5 days for CA mail). Keep proof of service. Consider a brief neutral cover letter that (a) cites the Rent Board rate and the unit's anniversary date, (b) shows the arithmetic, (c) notes the tenant's right to call the Rent Board (415-252-4600) for free verification, (d) does not demand agreement or waiver of rights.
    Sources: [S07] California Civil Code § 827 — Notice of rent increase (state notice periods); [S08] California Code of Civil Procedure § 1013 — Service by mail (+5 days in-state)
  8. Step 8: After the effective date, accept rent at the new amount and maintain records
    Keep the notice, proof of service, license confirmation, rate sheet, and banking worksheet in the tenancy file. If a tenant files a petition, respond factually and on time.
    Sources: [S01] SF.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco; [S14] SF Rent Board Rules and Regulations, Part 4 — Annual Increases (banking Rule 4.12)
Common small-owner mistakes to avoid
  • Skipping the Housing Inventory because 'I only have two units.' The law applies to you.
  • Using next year's percentage because you served notice this year. Use the rate in effect on the effective date.
  • Rounding the dollar amount up. Don't.
  • Serving a large banked increase with 30 days' notice. Anything over 10% (including cumulative increases in the prior 12 months) needs 90 days.
  • Forgetting to add 5 days when the notice is mailed within California.
  • Charging extra for the new roommate in Unit 1. SF explicitly forbids that.
