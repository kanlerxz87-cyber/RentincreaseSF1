Rent Board License & Housing Inventory — Your top question, answered

You asked: “I am just an individual property owner and not part of a corporation, so do I still need to apply for the license and inform the rent board before notifying tenants of a rent increase?”

Short answer: Yes. There is no individual-owner or small-building exemption. Reporting is required of ALL owners of residential property in San Francisco, and the license must be in effect on the effective date of any annual or banked increase.

Do I, as an individual small-property owner (not a corporation), need to apply for a rent-increase license / inform the Rent Board before notifying tenants of a rent increase?Yes — absolutely. The Housing Inventory reporting requirement and rent-increase license apply to ALL owners of residential property in San Francisco, including individuals and buildings with fewer than 10 units. There is no individual/small-owner exemption.

Per the SF Rent Board: 'If you own residential property, you are required to report into the Housing Inventory online through the Rent Board Portal. Property owners who have tenants will receive a rent increase license after the submission.' For buildings with fewer than 10 residential units (which covers this 2-unit building), the requirement took effect March 1, 2023. The license must be in effect on the effective date of the increase. An increase imposed while unlicensed is null and void, regardless of whether the percentage arithmetic is correct. Licenses are issued automatically when you report a tenant-occupied unit — there is no separate 'application' beyond the Housing Inventory filing.

Does being an individual (not a corporation) change whether I need the license?No. The rule draws no individual-vs-corporation distinction. The threshold is building size (10+ units started 7/1/2022; 1–9 units and condos started 3/1/2023), not owner entity type.

Individual owners of 2-unit, 3-unit, single-family-home and condo rentals all must report by March 1 each year. Corporate/LLC ownership matters for OTHER exemptions (e.g., the Costa-Hawkins single-family-home exemption is unavailable to certain corporate owners), but it does not affect the licensing requirement.

Is there a separate license application, or is reporting to the Housing Inventory enough?Reporting is what generates the license. There is no separate 'rent-increase license application.'

The Rent Board states: 'Once you have reported into the Housing Inventory, a rent increase license will be automatically generated for non-owner occupied units.' You confirm the license is in effect before serving notice. You do not need to attach the license to the tenant notice.
Sources: [S02] SF.gov — The Rent Board Housing Inventory (FAQ)
What happens if I serve a notice without an active license?The entire increase is null and void. The tenant is not obligated to pay it, and any overpayment is recoverable by the tenant.

Per Ordinance §37.15(d)–(e): during any period of noncompliance the license is suspended, and an annual or banked increase imposed in that period is unenforceable. This applies even if the percentage and notice period are correct.

Why the rule applies to you

Ordinance 265-20 (effective 1/18/2021) added Administrative Code §37.15. The SF Rent Board states:

  • For condominium units and units in buildings with fewer than 10 residential units (which covers your 2-unit building), the requirement took effect March 1, 2023.
  • Reporting is done at portal.sfrb.org.
  • When you report a non-owner-occupied (tenant-occupied) unit, a rent-increase license is automatically generated; there is no separate 'application'.
  • The license must be in effect on the effective date of the increase, not just when you file.
  • There is no monetary penalty for late filing, but an increase imposed while unlicensed is null and void — the tenant does not have to pay it.
