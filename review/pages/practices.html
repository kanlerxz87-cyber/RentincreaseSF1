How corporate vs. individual owners handle increases

Note: Descriptions of corporate practices here rest on public regulatory materials (SF Admin. Code §37.10C algorithmic-pricing ban, DOJ RealPage litigation reporting, Census/HUD Rental Housing Finance Survey categories) — no proprietary or anecdotal corporate data is used.

Large corporate owners / REITs

Large corporate owners and REITs typically: (1) centralise Housing Inventory filings through property-management software and confirm license status before every notice; (2) apply the annual published percentage on every anniversary across their portfolio, banking and drawing down per Rules 4.10–4.12; (3) use standard written notices served by mail with proof of service; (4) pursue capital-improvement and operating-and-maintenance petitions where justified; (5) maintain detailed rent ledgers; (6) avoid algorithmic 'revenue-management' tools that rely on non-public competitor data — San Francisco Administrative Code §37.10C prohibits that practice. NOTE: Specific corporate practices are described from public regulatory materials; no proprietary corporate data is used.

Individual San Francisco owners

Individual owners in San Francisco typically: (1) file the Housing Inventory themselves through the Rent Board Portal each March; (2) give the annual allowable increase (1.6% this year) on or shortly after the anniversary date; (3) either use banked increases modestly or phase them rather than impose the entire banked amount in one jump (for tenant-relations and to avoid the 90-day-notice trigger at >10%); (4) keep a simple worksheet showing each year's rate and whether it was taken; (5) consult the Rent Board's free counseling line or a tenant/landlord attorney when anything is unusual (roommate changes, passthroughs, O&M petitions).

What this means for you

  • The 'reasonable and normal' practice for an individual 2-unit Outer Sunset owner is the annual 1.6% on anniversary, with a clear written worksheet.
  • Do not deploy any algorithmic / revenue-management pricing tool that benchmarks against non-public competitor data — SF Admin. Code §37.10C prohibits that practice.
  • If you decide to use banked increases, phase them or at minimum itemise them year-by-year.
  • The Rent Board's free counseling line (415-252-4600 / 311) is available to both you and the tenant — expect the tenant to call.
