Laws

Federal, state and local requirements in one matrix, with what each one actually does to these two tenancies.

Which layer actually decides the number

Three layers apply at once, but only one sets the price. Federal law adds anti-discrimination duties and no rent cap. California's statewide cap is expressly displaced where a local ordinance is stricter, which is the case in San Francisco S14. The Rent Ordinance therefore decides: 1.6% now, once every twelve months, with a 7% absolute ceiling S08 S01. State law still adds the notice mechanics S15 and the anti-retaliation rule S18, and both state and federal law add the anti-discrimination rules S19 S20.

Note on the state cap figure

The statewide formula is “5% plus the percentage change in the cost of living, or 10%, whichever is lower” S14. The actual CPI-derived statewide percentage for a given year is published separately; it is not reproduced here because it does not apply to these units, and reproducing it without its own official citation would breach the no-unverified-values rule. Nothing on this site depends on it.

What “rent” means for the calculation

The Ordinance defines rent broadly — any consideration demanded for use or occupancy, including parking and furnishings — and treats a reduction in housing services without a corresponding rent reduction as a rent increase S08. The Rent Board's calculation instruction is narrower: apply the percentage to base rent, including housing services such as parking or storage, excluding temporary passthroughs and fluctuating charges S01. A rent increase licence does not change the amount that may be charged; it only determines whether the increase can take effect S09.
