F1 · Banking look-back: 12 months or 24 months?

What the regulation says

"A landlord who refrains from imposing an annual rent increase, or any portion thereof, may accumulate said increase and impose that amount on or after the tenant's subsequent rent increase anniversary date … A full 12 months must have elapsed from the date that an annual rent increase, or a portion thereof, could have been imposed before this banking section becomes applicable." — Rule 4.12(a), S9

What the explainer page says

"A skipped increase is only banked if at least 24 full months pass without any rent increase. If less than 24 months pass, the skipped increase is lost and cannot be imposed at a later date." — Rent Board, S10

Why it matters. Unit 1's first allowance became available on 1 February 2026 at 1.4%. Under Rule 4.12, twelve months elapse on 1 February 2027, so the 1.4% could be added to the 2027 notice, producing $119.85 (3.0%). Under the 24-month reading, the 1.4% would still be unavailable in February 2027, and only the 1.6% ($63.92) may be served.

What is not in doubt. Unit 2's banked years are all more than 24 months old except the 1 June 2026 annual allowance, which is used as the current annual increase rather than as a banked one — so the 13.1% figure is unaffected by this flag.

Resolution needed. Ask a Rent Board counsellor (415-252-4600) which look-back applies, and record the answer with the date. Until then, serve the 1.6% only. Needs review

F2 · Was the April 2026 change a partial or a complete change of occupancy?

The record supplied says the original lease named three tenants, that two remain, and that a new occupant joined. On that basis the change is partial: §37.3(d)(2)(B) disapplies the vacancy reset where "one or more of the occupants … remains an occupant in lawful possession", and §37.3(a)(11)(A) forbids an increase for an added occupant. If, in fact, all three original tenants vacated and the "continuing" tenants are all new, the analysis changes completely: a new tenancy may be priced at market under Costa-Hawkins, subject to §37.3(d)/(f) restrictions (S23, S4).

Resolution needed. Confirm the occupancy dates of each named tenant from the lease, any roommate agreement, and the rent ledger. Needs review

F4 · Rent-increase licence not confirmed

"If the landlord has not substantially complied with the reporting obligation, then the license to impose rent increases shall be temporarily suspended … and a tenant shall not be obligated to pay the increased rent for months during the period of suspension." — §37.15(e), S5. The Rent Board adds: "your rent increase notice will not be enforceable without having a rent increase license on the effective date of the notice" (S13).

Resolution needed. Check both units in the Rent Board Portal and save a dated screenshot. Reporting for buildings of 1–9 units began on 1 March 2023, so a two-unit building had to be reported from that date. Blocking

F5 · The 2027–28 rate does not exist yet

The Rent Board's rate sheet lists published percentages only through 28 February 2027 (S2); the next figure is announced in the new year. Any increase with an effective date on or after 1 March 2027 must use the 2027–28 figure, which is Unverified and left blank here. This is why the whole schedule on the Notice page is built around a 28 February 2027 effective date. Unverified

F6 · Negative findings and blank values

  • No federal rent ceiling. No federal statute or regulation reviewed sets a residential rent ceiling for private housing. The Fair Housing Act governs discriminatory terms and conditions (S25), and HUD administers fair housing and subsidy programmes (S26). This is recorded as a negative finding, not as a positive citation.
  • Market rent left blank. A HUD Fair Market Rent figure for San Francisco County would have been a useful official benchmark, but the HUD Fair Market Rent documentation system returned no numeric values when queried for this report, and no substitute official figure was located. No market-rent number is asserted anywhere on this site. Unverified — blank
  • Unit characteristics left blank. Bedroom count and square footage were not supplied and are not published here; they do not affect the annual allowance but would matter to any rent-comparability argument.

F7 · Coverage is assumed, not documented

Coverage is inferred from the 1940 construction date: units with a certificate of occupancy on or before 13 June 1979 have rent control (S12). The Rent Board warns that "some units are hard to classify" and that a counsellor should be consulted if unsure. Two scenarios would change the answer: a unit converted to a separately alienable condominium or single-family dwelling with a tenancy beginning on or after 1 January 1996, or a unit that is exempt for another statutory reason.

Resolution needed. Confirm the building's certificate-of-occupancy date and the unit's legal configuration on the Planning Department's Property Information Map. Needs review

F8 · Value of the banked remainder after a partial increase

Option B on the Units page (1.6% annual plus 5.0 points of banked allowance) leaves 6.5 percentage points banked. The Rent Board's rule is that a partial increase banks the remainder (S10), and banked percentages are applied to the base rent in force when they are imposed. That means the dollar value of the remainder is not fixed today. No dollar figure is asserted for it; the percentage is what is preserved. Illustrative only

F9 · Subsidy status of either household is unknown

Section 37.3(a)(10) sets a different framework where a tenant holds a tenant-based rental assistance voucher: if the base rent is below the payment standard, the local increase limits may not apply in the same way, and adjustments may follow the subsidy programme. No information about subsidy status was supplied for either unit, and none is asserted here. Needs review

F10 · Repair and complaint history

Civil Code §1942.5 bars a rent increase within 180 days of a tenant's good-faith repair notice, an agency complaint, an inspection or a citation, where the tenant is not in default (S22). Section 37.10B separately makes bad-faith conduct actionable (S6). No maintenance or complaint records were supplied.

Resolution needed. Review the maintenance log, any 311 or Department of Building Inspection records, and tenant correspondence for the previous 12 months. If any complaint exists inside the window, defer the increase. Blocking

F11 · Conflicting third-party figures and unverified practice claims

  • The rate itself. Several commercial sites state the 2026–27 allowance as 1.7%. The Rent Board's rate sheet, announcement and "Learn about rent increases" page all state 1.6% (S2, S3, S1). The official 1.6% is used throughout; the discrepancy is recorded because a tenant could cite the wrong figure.
  • Practice claims. Statements about how large or small owners typically behave are not supported by any official dataset that was located. They appear on the Owner practice page marked Unverified with no value asserted.

F12 · Timing sensitivity for Unit 2

To use the 1.6% rate, Unit 2's increase must take effect on or before 28 February 2027. A 13.1% increase needs 90 days' notice, so service must occur by 30 November 2026 (25 November 2026 if mailed). If that date is missed, the effective date moves past 1 March 2027 and the rate becomes the unpublished 2027–28 figure (F5). A phased increase of 10% or less needs only 30 days, which keeps the deadline open until late January 2027.

Resolution needed. Choose the structure before 25 November 2026, or accept that the increase moves to the 2027–28 rate. Timing
