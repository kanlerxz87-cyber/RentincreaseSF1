Task T3 · local · state · federal

Three levels of law, one controlling number

San Francisco is a "home rule" rent-control city with limits stricter than the state statute. The state therefore steps aside on the rent cap but keeps its notice rules, its anti-retaliation rule and its civil-rights statute. Federal law sets no rent ceiling at all; what it does impose is a prohibition on discriminatory terms and conditions.

Why the federal row is a negative finding. A claim that "there is no federal rent control" cannot be proven by citing a statute that says so. What can be said, and is said here, is narrower: the federal materials reviewed for this report — the Fair Housing Act and HUD's fair-housing programme pages — contain no rent ceiling for private housing, and no other federal rent-setting authority was located. That is recorded as flag F6 rather than asserted as a positive fact.

Which level wins when they differ

  1. Local controls the number because its limit (1.6%) is stricter than the state's (8.8%), and state law expressly defers to stricter local controls (S20 §1947.12(d)(3)).
  2. State controls the notice mechanics. Civil Code §827's 30/90-day periods and service rules apply to San Francisco tenancies; the Rent Board's guidance repeats the same periods (S21, S1).
  3. Both state and federal civil-rights law control the "who". The widest protected-class list governs practical conduct: FEHA adds source of income, marital status, ancestry and more; §37.10B(a)(9) prohibits violating any anti-discrimination law as an act of harassment (S24, S6).
  4. Federal law adds nothing to the amount but supplies an independent claim if a tenant believes the increase was applied on a protected ground (S25).
