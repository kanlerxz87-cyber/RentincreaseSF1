Reconciliation

What the three published editions of this analysis agree on, and where they legitimately differ.

Three agent sessions worked this repository independently on 2026-11-06 and all three reached the same core numbers. That is useful corroboration, but it also means there is more than one published site. This page records what agrees, what differs, and where a reader should look for what. Nothing from the earlier editions was deleted or overwritten.

The headline

Two independent analyses of the same primary sources arrived at identical figures to the cent — $63.92, $119.85, $412.65, $4,058.92, $4,114.85, $3,562.65 — identical notice periods, and independently flagged the same two substantive uncertainties. Nothing on this page changes a number.

Open in both editions

These are factual gaps in the owner's records, not disagreements about the law. Neither edition fills them in.

  • Whether the building's certificate of occupancy date and dwelling-unit count confirm rent-control coverage.
  • Whether any rent increase has actually been imposed on Unit 2 since June 1, 2019. Both editions treat 'none' as an assumption and flag it.
  • Whether the owner holds a current rent increase licence.
  • Whether $3,995 and $3,150 are entirely base rent.
  • Whether the tenant added to Unit 1 is a co-tenant or a subtenant.
  • Whether any repair complaint, inspection, organising activity or buyout discussion occurred in the last 180 days.
  • A verified ZIP-level market rent. Neither edition uses one.

Conclusion

Two independent analyses of the same primary sources produced identical dollar figures to the cent, identical notice periods, and independently identified the same two substantive uncertainties. That is the strongest evidence available here that the numbers are right. The remaining open items are factual gaps in the owner's own records, not disagreements about the law.
