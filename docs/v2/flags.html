FLAG-02 high

Unit 1 anniversary date is ambiguous between February 1 and April 1

The original lease began February 1, 2025. A new tenancy is recorded as beginning April 1, 2026 at the same $3,995. The Rent Board defines the anniversary date as the date of the last rent increase, or, if there has never been an increase, the date the lease began. If the April 1, 2026 change is treated as a new lease, the anniversary date could be read as April 1.

Why it matters
It controls the earliest effective date of any increase and therefore which annual rate applies. It also controls whether the February 1, 2026 annual increase can be banked.
Effect on the numbers
Two readings are presented. Reading A (February 1): 1.6% = $63.92, or 3.0% = $119.85 with the banked 1.4%. Reading B (April 1): the safest available increase is 1.6%, and the banked 1.4% becomes available only after April 1, 2028.
What would resolve it
Ask a Rent Board counsellor (415-252-4600) which date controls where the rent did not change but the named tenants did. Do not assume the more favourable reading.

FLAG-03 high

Unit 2: it is an assumption that no increase has been taken since June 1, 2019

The briefing gives the June 1, 2019 lease rate of $3,150 and no later rate. The 13.1% figure depends entirely on no increase having been imposed since then, because each imposed increase resets the anniversary date and breaks the banking chain.

Why it matters
The 24-month banking test is what makes $412.65 available. One properly noticed increase anywhere in the chain reduces the total.
Effect on the numbers
If any increase was taken, 13.1% is overstated and the lawful maximum is lower. The exact figure cannot be computed until the rent history is produced.
What would resolve it
Produce the rent ledger and every notice served since 2019 for Unit 2, and recompute. The Rent Board can also determine the lawful rent (S20).

FLAG-04 high

Unit 2's 13.1% expires as a maximum on February 28, 2027

The 1.6% annual component is the rate for 3/1/26-2/28/27. A June 1 anniversary falls after that date, so a June 1, 2027 increase would use the 2027-28 rate, which the Rent Board has not yet announced. The banked 13.1% minus that component (11.5%) remains banked and does not expire, but the total cannot be stated until the new rate is published.

Why it matters
Someone may plan a June 1, 2027 increase using 13.1% and be wrong in either direction.
Effect on the numbers
The 2027-28 components are left blank on the Calculations page.
What would resolve it
Watch for the Rent Board's annual announcement each January (S02 is the equivalent 2026 announcement) and recompute after publication.

FLAG-09 high

Rent increase licence status for this building is unknown

A landlord must hold a rent increase licence, obtained by reporting into the Rent Board Housing Inventory, before an annual or banked increase can take effect. Reporting is annual by March 1, and for buildings with fewer than 10 units it began on March 1, 2023. Whether this owner has reported is not known.

Why it matters
A notice served without a licence in effect on the effective date is unenforceable, and the tenant is not obligated to pay the increase for the period of suspension. This defect voids an otherwise perfectly calculated increase.
Effect on the numbers
None on the arithmetic; it can void the increase entirely.
What would resolve it
Check the property on the Rent Board Portal (portal.sfrb.org) or report if not already done, and keep a screenshot of the licence status dated before the notice is served.

FLAG-01 medium

Year built (1940) is owner-supplied and was not independently confirmed

Rent control in San Francisco attaches to units in buildings with a certificate of occupancy on or before June 13, 1979. 1940 is far before that date, so coverage on age grounds is not in doubt. However, this session did not query the Assessor or the Planning Department Property Information Map for the actual permit or assessor record.

Why it matters
If a later permit created a new unit, or if either unit is legally a single-family dwelling, the coverage analysis changes. A single-family dwelling tenancy begun on or after January 1, 1996 can be partially exempt under Costa-Hawkins.
Effect on the numbers
None. Coverage is treated as applying to both units.
What would resolve it
Look up the address on the SF Planning Property Information Map (S32) and the Assessor's records, and record the certificate-of-occupancy date and the dwelling-unit count.

FLAG-06 medium

Whether $3,995 and $3,150 are entirely base rent is unconfirmed

The calculation must be made on base rent only, which can include parking or storage but excludes temporary passthroughs and fluctuating charges. The briefing gives a single monthly figure for each unit and does not say whether it includes a passthrough or a separate parking charge.

Why it matters
If any part of the stated rent is a passthrough, the base rent is lower and the dollar increase is lower.
Effect on the numbers
Could reduce the dollar amounts. Percentages are unaffected.
What would resolve it
Check each lease and the most recent notices for any itemised passthrough, and confirm the base rent figure before calculating.

FLAG-07 medium

Unit 1: the identity and role of the added tenant is unknown

Unit 1 moved from three named tenants to two named tenants including one who was not on the initial lease. Whether that person is a co-tenant or a subtenant determines whether an unlimited increase route under Sec. 6.14 / Costa-Hawkins could ever apply, and whether a 6.14 notice should have been served.

Why it matters
A co-tenant is not subject to an unlimited increase regardless of when the co-tenant moved in. A subtenant can be, but only if a written 6.14 notice was served within a reasonable time, generally 60 days, after the landlord learned of the occupancy.
Effect on the numbers
None on the figures shown, because the unlimited route is not assumed to be available. It matters if you were considering anything above the annual amount.
What would resolve it
Establish whether Tenant D signed the lease or moved in as a subtenant, and whether any 6.14 notice was ever served. If none was served, do not rely on that route.

FLAG-10 medium

No information on repair complaints, inspections, organising or buyout discussions

The review tests for retaliation, harassment and the algorithmic-pricing ban cannot be assessed because no facts were supplied on repair complaints, DBI or health inspections, tenant association activity, or any prior buyout or offer-to-vacate discussion.

Why it matters
A rent increase within 180 days of a tenantability complaint is separately prohibited regardless of the amount. An increase within six months of an attempt to recover possession is expressly listed as evidence of bad faith. Either can convert a lawful amount into a violation.
Effect on the numbers
None on the arithmetic. Can make the increase unlawful.
What would resolve it
Confirm in writing that none of those events occurred in the 180 days preceding the notice, and record that confirmation with the file.

Query the HUD FMR documentation system with the HUD Metro FMR Area parameter, or the Census ACS table B25064 for ZCTA 94122, and record the figure with its retrieval date.

FLAG-11 low

Third-party sites publish a conflicting 1.7% figure for the same period

Several commercial rent calculators and blogs state that the 2026-27 allowable increase is 1.7%, and one states that the 2025-26 figure was 1.4% while the current one is 1.7%. The Rent Board's own pages and its official PDF both state 1.6% for 3/1/26-2/28/27 and 1.4% for 3/1/25-2/28/26. The 1.7% figure corresponds to 3/1/24-2/28/25 and appears to be an off-by-one-year error propagated between sites.

Why it matters
Using 1.7% instead of 1.6% would overstate the lawful increase. On Unit 1 that is the difference between $63.92 and $67.92.
Effect on the numbers
None. 1.6% is used throughout, from the official source.
What would resolve it
None needed. Third-party figures are excluded from all calculations. Recorded here so the conflict is visible rather than silently resolved.

FLAG-12 low

The 2026-27 rate itself is an estimate, not a straight CPI reading

October 2025 CPI was never published because of the 2025 lapse in federal appropriations. The Bureau of Labor Statistics confirms the missing value on its own release. The Rent Board therefore estimated the missing October 2025 CPI to preserve its October-to-October method, producing 1.6%. A similar approach is expected for the 2027-28 figure.

Why it matters
It explains why sources disagree, and it means the 2027-28 rate may be computed by an unusual method. It does not make 1.6% challengeable: it is the published, in-force rate.
Effect on the numbers
None. 1.6% is the operative rate until February 28, 2027.
What would resolve it
None. Monitor the January announcement for the following year.
