Tenant-lawyer review

Fourteen questions a San Francisco tenant advocate would ask, with a pass condition for each.

Each row is a question a San Francisco tenant lawyer or Rent Board counsellor would ask about a proposed increase. If every row passes, the increase is in the normal range and procedurally clean. A failure on any row is the kind of defect that leads to a Rent Board petition.

This is a self-check built from the cited authorities. It is not legal advice and it is not a substitute for advice from a lawyer or a Rent Board counsellor.

Unit 2

Passes on the cited authorities at 13.1% only if four conditions hold: no increase since June 1, 2019; effective on or before February 28, 2027; 90 days' notice; a current rent increase licence. Any one of those failing makes the increase defective or void.

What happens if a tenant does petition

This is not a threat to be avoided; it is a process to be anticipated. Filing is free, there is no time limit, and the hearing is designed so that no one needs a lawyer.

  • The tenant can file a Tenant Petition (Form 516C) free of charge, with no time limit (S20).
  • If the notice is clearly unlawful on its face and has not yet taken effect, the tenant can file a Tenant Summary Petition (Form 518) (S22).
  • A tenant who received the notice within the last 60 days and whose landlord has not made required repairs can file Form 516B (S22).
  • The hearing is before a Rent Board Administrative Law Judge and is designed so that no one needs an attorney (S20).
  • The Rent Board does not adjudicate harassment, discrimination or retaliation; those go to court or another forum (S21).

The single strongest defensive document

Not the notice. The notice only proves what you told the tenant. What answers a harassment or discrimination allegation is a short file note, written at the time, that records: the written policy; each unit's rent history and anniversary date; the calculation with each year's percentage; the date the decision was taken; the notice served and the service method; and confirmation that no repair complaint, inspection, organising activity or buyout discussion preceded it. A reviewer who can reproduce both numbers from the Rent Board's published table, and who sees the same rule applied to both units, has nothing to argue with.
