The five questions, answered

Each answer with the authority behind it, and a plain statement where the law is silent.

Five questions were asked. Each is answered below with the authority it rests on. Where the honest answer is "the law does not say", that is stated rather than guessed.

What "normal" means in practice. A normal increase in this city is the annual percentage applied on the tenant's anniversary date. Anything above the annual plus banked amount requires a Rent Board petition filed before the notice is served, and there are only six such routes. S12 A below-market long tenancy is explicitly not one of them. S14

3. What is the correct way to tell tenants, and what are reasonable reasons?

How: a written notice stating the dollar amount, the percentage, the effective date, and which portion is annual and which is banked, served 30 days ahead (90 days if the increase exceeds 10%), plus 5 days if mailed. Then the licence must already be in place. The full sequence is on Notice & procedure. S08 S01 S24 S09

The pattern is simple: the lawful reasons are all mechanical, documentary and impersonal — a published percentage, a documented expense, a certified improvement. Every reason that turns on who the tenant is, how long they have stayed, or how much the market would bear is either unavailable or requires a petition.

4. Would this survive review by a San Francisco tenant lawyer?

That question has a concrete answer, because the review is cheap and the remedies are asymmetric: filing a Rent Board petition is free and has no time limit, and the Rent Board has no power to refuse a filing. S20 So the right way to think about it is not "will we win" but "can a reviewer find a defect".

The Tenant-lawyer review page turns that into 14 specific questions with a pass condition for each. On the facts supplied, both proposed increases pass on the arithmetic. Four items cannot be assessed at all, because they depend on facts not provided: the licence status, whether $3,995 and $3,150 are entirely base rent, whether any complaint or inspection occurred in the last 180 days, and whether any pricing tool was used.

The one distinction worth being precise about. Free legal representation in San Francisco attaches through the Tenant Right to Counsel program, which is triggered when a tenant faces eviction, not when a rent increase notice is served. S33 The rent-increase route is a free Rent Board petition, which is designed so that no one needs a lawyer. S20 Both matter, but they are different processes.

5. One unit or both?

Raise both units, under one written policy — not by the same percentage. The two tenancies are not similarly situated: Unit 1's tenancy began in 2025, Unit 2's in 2019 with six skipped increases banked. Discrimination law asks whether the basis for a difference is a protected characteristic, not whether two tenants received identical numbers. S28 S30

ConsiderationFindingSource
The two tenancies are not similarly situatedUnit 1's tenancy began in 2025 and has one skipped annual increase available at most. Unit 2's tenancy began in 2019 and has six skipped annual increases plus the current one. Applying the same percentage to both would be arbitrary; applying each tenancy's own lawful maximum is the consistent rule.S04
A uniform percentage is not required by any source reviewedNothing in Chapter 37, the Civil Code, FEHA, the Fair Housing Act or 24 CFR 100 requires a landlord to raise every tenant by the same percentage or at the same time. The requirement is that the basis not be a protected characteristic.S08
What actually creates discrimination exposureGovernment Code Sec. 12955(a) and 42 U.S.C. Sec. 3604(b) reach discrimination in the terms, conditions or privileges of rental because of a protected characteristic. Source of income, familial status, disability, marital status and national origin are all protected in California. If a decision to raise one rent and not another correlates with any of those, the exposure is real regardless of whether the amounts were lawful.S28
The protective practice is a written, uniform policyThe defensible position is a written rule applied identically: for every rent-controlled unit, the annual allowable increase is imposed on the anniversary date; banked increases are imposed only when the 24-month test is met. Under that rule Unit 2 receives 13.1% and Unit 1 receives 1.6% or 3.0%, and the difference is fully explained by the rule itself.S04
Skipping a unit is permitted but has consequencesA landlord may impose less than the maximum, or nothing at all. Partial use banks the remainder. Declining to raise Unit 1 today does not waive the increase forever, but if no increase is imposed for 24 months the intervening annual increases are lost rather than banked.S04
Retaliation and harassment are the larger risk, not unequal amountsSec. 37.10B(a)(9) treats violating anti-discrimination law as harassment, and Sec. 37.10B(a)(5) treats an increase imposed to push a tenant out as harassment. The exposure is conduct and motive, not the arithmetic difference between two units.S11
Documentation is the practical defenceRecord: the written policy, the calculation for each unit, the date of the decision, the notice served and the service method. A tenant lawyer reviewing the file should be able to reconstruct both numbers from the Rent Board's own published table without asking you a question.S08

Do

  • Raise both units under one written policy, not both by the same percentage.
  • For Unit 2 the lawful maximum is 13.1% ($412.65) if effective on or before February 28, 2027, served with 90 days' notice.
  • For Unit 1 the lawful maximum is 1.6% ($63.92) effective February 1, 2027, or 3.0% ($119.85) on the same date if the February 1 anniversary reading and the 24-month test both hold.
  • %s

Don't

  • Do not impose the same dollar or percentage amount on both units by using a figure that exceeds either unit's lawful ceiling.
  • Do not use market rent or a pricing tool as the basis for either number.
  • Do not raise Unit 1 because of the April 2026 occupant change.
  • Do not serve a 13.1% notice with 30 days' notice.
