Owner practice

What the law requires of every owner, and an explicit statement of what could not be sourced about behaviour.

The brief asked how large corporations and how individual owners handle increases. The honest answer has two halves: the law treats them identically, and no source located describes their behaviour. Both halves are reported.

Read this before the table

  • No official dataset records how San Francisco owners set rents in practice. No source located in this session documents institutional or individual owner behaviour with evidence. The 'practice' rows below are therefore left blank and marked unverified rather than filled in from assumption.
  • The legal ceiling is identical whoever the owner is. Nothing in this section changes the 1.6% / banked analysis on the Calculations page.
  • California's statewide Tenant Protection Act covers many corporate owners, but in San Francisco the local 1.6% is stricter, so the local rule controls (S25, Sec. 1947.12(d)(3)). A common misconception is that a larger owner may use the state cap instead. It may not, in this city.

Why the "big corporations do it differently" idea does not help here

Three specific claims are sometimes made, and each fails against the sources:

ClaimWhat the law actually saysSource
Large owners may use the state cap of about 8.8% instead of the city's 1.6%.No. Civil Code §1947.12(d)(3) exempts housing subject to a local ordinance that restricts increases to a lower amount. The local cap controls.S25
Large owners may use revenue-management software to set rents.No, not in San Francisco since October 14, 2024. Sec. 37.10C prohibits selling or using algorithmic devices that use non-public competitor data to recommend rent or occupancy.S15
Large owners can push rents up to market on long tenancies.No. A long-standing tenant paying below market is expressly not an extraordinary circumstance that justifies an above-cap increase.S14

Where this leaves the reader: the question "how do corporations do it" has a legal answer (the same way) and a factual answer that is unverified. The legal answer is the one that protects you.
