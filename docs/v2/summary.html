Executive summary

The answer, the reasoning and the recommended action — in one page.

Bottom line

Both units are covered by the San Francisco Rent Ordinance because the building predates June 13, 1979. The lawful ceiling is the Rent Board's published annual increase plus any properly banked increases, and nothing else. On these facts the ceilings are $63.92–$119.85 per month for Unit 1 and $412.65 per month for Unit 2. Raising rent on one unit and not the other is lawful and, given these facts, is the logical result of the law; doing so under a single written policy is what removes the discrimination risk.

Findings, in order of importance

  1. The local cap controls, and it is small. 1.6% for March 1, 2026 through February 28, 2027, set at 60% of the published Bay Area CPI increase. S02 S03 S08
  2. The state cap does not apply to these units. AB 1482's 5% + CPI ceiling is expressly displaced where a local ordinance caps increases lower. S25
  3. No federal law caps rent. Federal law bars discrimination in the terms of rental, which is a different question. S29 S30
  4. Unit 1's ceiling is $63.92, or $119.85 if the February anniversary date holds. A building with three tenants becoming two does not create a legal basis for a larger increase, and an added occupant cannot be charged for. S01 S04 S16
  5. Unit 2's ceiling is $412.65 (13.1%) — but only until February 28, 2027, and only with 90 days' notice. The 13.1% is the sum of six banked annual increases plus the current one, added and never compounded. S03 S04 S24
  6. A long tenancy below market rent is not a lawful reason to raise rent. The Rent Board says so in terms: it is not an extraordinary circumstance. S14
  7. The rent increase licence is the most likely failure point. An otherwise perfect increase is unenforceable without a licence in effect on the effective date. S09 S10
  8. Treating the units differently is defensible; treating them by a protected characteristic is not. The legal test is whether the basis is a protected characteristic, not whether the amounts match. S28 S30 S11
  9. Any increase should be assumed to be reviewed for free. Rent Board petitions are free with no time limit, and free legal representation exists at the eviction stage. Build the file so a reviewer can reproduce both numbers from the Rent Board's own table. S20 S33

Recommended course of action

Unit 1

Serve a 30-day notice (35 if mailed) for an increase of 1.6% → $63.92, effective February 1, 2027. If a Rent Board counsellor confirms the February 1 anniversary date controls, 3.0% → $119.85 is also lawful on the same date.

Do not increase this unit because of the April 2026 occupant change.

Unit 2

Serve a 90-day notice (95 if mailed) for 13.1% → $412.65, effective on or before February 28, 2027, itemising each banked year. Alternatively serve the annual 1.6% only.

Before serving, confirm no increase has been taken since June 1, 2019 — the whole figure depends on it.

The one procedural rule that binds both: report into the Rent Board Housing Inventory and confirm the rent increase licence is active before the notice is served. S09 S10
