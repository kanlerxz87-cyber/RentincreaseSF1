Task T4 · procedure and justification

The notice is where most increases fail — not the percentage

A correctly calculated increase can still be void if the notice is late, missing a component, served the wrong way, or issued without a rent-increase licence. San Francisco treats notice defects strictly: a non-conforming increase "shall be null and void".

Lawful versus unlawful reasons

The question "what is a reasonable reason for the increase?" has a shorter answer than most owners expect. For a sitting tenant in a covered unit, the reason is the published allowance itself; the ordinance is a price-control scheme, not a reasons-based one. Market rent is not a lawful reason, and no justification letter is required for the annual or banked portion.

Sequence to follow, in order

  1. Confirm coverage. Certificate of occupancy on or before 13 June 1979 (S12). For a 1940 building this is settled unless a unit has been converted to a separately alienable condominium or used as a single-family dwelling in the statutory sense — flag F7.
  2. Confirm the licence. Check the unit in the Rent Board Portal; the increase is unenforceable without a licence on the effective date, and the tenant need not pay for the suspended months (S5, S13).
  3. Fix the base rent and the anniversary date from records. Base rent is the lawful rent in force, not the last amount requested. Keep the lease, prior notices and payment record (S1).
  4. List every unused anniversary and its published rate. Add the percentages; do not compound; do not round up (S9, S11).
  5. Decide the effective date and work backwards. 30 or 90 days, plus five if mailing (S21).
  6. Draft the notice with the itemisation. Annual portion, banked portion with years, effective date, dollar and percentage amounts (Article 6 of the table above).
  7. Serve and evidence service. Personal service with a signed receipt, or mail with proof of mailing. Keep copies for the life of the tenancy.
  8. File the fee and keep the inventory current. $59 per unit for 2026–27, due 1 March; any 50% recovery from tenants must be a separate fee recovery, not rent (S14).
  9. Tell the tenant how to check it. Point them to the Rent Board (415-252-4600) and to forms 516C and 518. A tenant who can verify the number is a tenant who does not need a lawyer to challenge it (S18, S16).

Illustrative notice skeleton

This is a drafting aid, not an official form. The Rent Board publishes the authoritative rate sheet and forms; a Rent Board counsellor will check a draft. Nothing below adds a requirement — it only arranges the required elements so that a reviewer can follow the arithmetic.

To: Tenant 1A and Tenant 1B, Unit 1, [street address withheld from this site]

Notice of rent increase — effective 1 February 2027

  • Current base rent: $3,995.00 per month.
  • Amount of increase: $63.92 per month (1.6%).
  • Basis: annual allowable increase of 1.6% for the period 1 March 2026 – 28 February 2027, published by the San Francisco Rent Board, applied to your anniversary date of 1 February.
  • Banked portion: none. (If the 1.4% from 1 February 2026 is claimed, state "$55.93, representing the 1.4% annual increase available on 1 February 2026 and not imposed".)
  • New base rent: $4,058.92 per month, effective 1 February 2027.
  • No rounding has been applied. No passthrough is included in this increase.
  • You may verify this calculation, free of charge, with the San Francisco Rent Board on 415-252-4600, and you may file a tenant petition with the Rent Board if you believe the increase is unlawful.

For Unit 2 under option C, the banked paragraph must itemise each year: 1.8% (1 June 2020), 0.7% (1 June 2021), 2.3% (1 June 2022), 3.6% (1 June 2023), 1.7% (1 June 2024), 1.4% (1 June 2025), plus the 1.6% annual increase for 1 June 2026 — total 13.1%, $412.65, new rent $3,562.65.

Mistakes that void or weaken an increase

  • VoidsServing before the licence exists (S5).
  • VoidsOmitting the annual/banked itemisation (S9 Rule 4.10(b)).
  • VoidsUsing the percentage in force when the notice was written rather than the one in force on the effective date (S1).
  • VoidsRounding the new rent up (S1).
  • VoidsCompounding banked percentages (S9).
  • VoidsCharging more because an occupant was added (S4).
  • WeakensToo little notice, or mailing without adding the five days (S21).
  • WeakensNo proof of service, so the effective date cannot be proved.
  • WeakensBundling a passthrough into the base rent — passthroughs must remain separate and are not part of base rent (S4).
