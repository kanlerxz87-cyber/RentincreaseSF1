Task T6 · one unit or both

Raising one unit and not the other is lawful. Raising it because of who lives there is not.

The question assumes a rule of parity between tenants. There is none — and there cannot be, because the ordinance itself produces different lawful rents in identical flats: different base rents, different anniversary dates, different banking histories. The legal risk lies elsewhere: in discrimination on a protected ground, in retaliation, and in bad-faith pressure to vacate.

Short answer. You may increase Unit 1, or Unit 2, or both, or neither. Each increase stands or falls on its own arithmetic and its own notice. What you should not do is let the choice be explained by anything other than rent history — and you should be able to prove that with records.

What is definitely unlawful

  • UnlawfulIncreasing rent because of race, national origin, religion, sex, gender identity or expression, sexual orientation, familial status, disability, marital status, source of income, age or immigration status — under FHA §804(b), FEHA §12955(a) and §37.10B(a)(9).
  • UnlawfulIncreasing rent to influence a tenant to vacate "through fraud, intimidation or coercion", including a rent increase "within six months after an attempt to recover possession" (§37.10B(a)(5)).
  • UnlawfulIncreasing rent within 180 days of a repair complaint, agency complaint, inspection or citation, where the tenant is not in default (Civil Code §1942.5).
  • UnlawfulIncreasing rent above the annual allowance plus banked amounts plus certified passthroughs, without a Rent Board petition (§37.3(a)(8)).
  • UnlawfulCharging more because an additional occupant joined the household (§37.3(a)(11)).
  • UnlawfulSetting rent using revenue-management software fed with non-public competitor data (§37.10C).

The one thing to write down before serving anything. A short dated policy note — "annual increases are applied at the Rent Board's published allowance on each tenancy's anniversary date; banked amounts are applied at the owner's discretion and itemised in the notice; no increase is based on the identity or circumstances of the occupants" — converts a discretionary decision into an auditable practice. Keep the calculation for each unit next to it. That is the artefact a tenant lawyer would ask for, and the artefact that answers the discrimination question without argument.
