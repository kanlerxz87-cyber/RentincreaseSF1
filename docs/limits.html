Task T2 · the published ceiling

The ceiling is published once a year, and it is small

San Francisco does not negotiate the annual increase. The Rent Board computes it from the Bay Area consumer price index, publishes it every 1 March, and every covered tenancy in the city shares the same percentage. For increases effective between 1 March 2026 and 28 February 2027 that figure is 1.6%.

How the 1.6% is derived — in the Board's own words

"On March 1 of each year, the Board shall publish the increase in the CPI for the preceding 12 months, as made available by the U.S. Department of Labor. A landlord who has reported the required information about their rental unit to the Rent Board as set forth in Section 37.15 shall have a license to impose annually a rent increase which does not exceed a tenant's base rent by more than 60% of said published increase. In no event, however, shall the allowable annual increase be greater than 7%." — §37.3(a)(1)

Two things follow. First, the increase is a licence conditioned on Housing Inventory reporting — the licence is dealt with on the Notice & reasons page. Second, the 7% figure is a backstop that has not bound in any recent year; the series below has stayed between 0.7% and 3.6% since 2019.

Local versus state: why 8.8% does not apply here

The statewide Tenant Protection Act (AB 1482, codified at Civil Code §1947.12) caps increases at 5% plus CPI, or 10%, whichever is lower. It does not apply to "housing subject to rent or price control through a public entity's valid exercise of its police power … that restricts annual increases in the rental rate to an amount less than that provided in subdivision (a)" (Civil Code §1947.12(d)(3), S20). The Rent Board says the same thing in terms a tenant or owner will recognise: AB 1482's 8.8% maximum for 1 August 2026 – 31 July 2027 "does NOT apply for units under the rent increase limitations of the San Francisco Rent Ordinance" (S17).

Because both units are in a 1940 building, the local limit is the operative one. The 8.8% figure would only be relevant to a unit that is outside the local rent-increase limits — for example a separately alienable single-family home or condominium with a tenancy that began on or after 1 January 1996 (S12).

The 10% number is a notice threshold, not a permission. Crossing 10% in any 12 months does not unlock a bigger increase; it only converts a 30-day notice into a 90-day notice under Civil Code §827 (S21). The lawful ceiling stays at the annual allowance plus properly banked amounts plus any certified passthroughs.
