Task T6 · adversarial review

Would a tenant-side lawyer find these increases reasonable and lawful?

San Francisco tenants have free and low-cost representation, a Rent Board petition process that costs nothing to use, and forms specifically designed to attack rent increases. The right way to test a notice is to run the review a tenant attorney would run, in their order, before serving it.

What a tenant can do, free. File form 516C for a determination that a rent increase is unlawful or a review of the rent history; file form 518 where a notice is void on its face and has not yet taken effect; challenge passthroughs with form 516D; ask the Rent Board to reduce rent for decreased services with form 516A. Counsel is available through the Rent Board's referral list, which includes a Sunset office of Open Door Legal at 1722 Irving Street, 94122. Eviction defence is separately funded through the City's Tenant Right to Counsel programme. Sources: S18, S16, S28.

The arguments a tenant attorney would actually make

Likely challengeStrengthHow the record answers it
"13.1% is an excessive increase."Weak on law, strong on opticsIt is the arithmetic sum of the Board's own published rates for seven unused anniversaries. A petition would test the calculation, not the size — unless the increase is shown to be in bad faith under §37.10B(a)(5).
"The increase was never approved by the Rent Board."WrongAnnual and banked increases do not require Board approval; only amounts above them do (S4).
"You cannot bank increases without telling us each year."WrongBanking is automatic on refraining; the notice must identify the banked portion and the years (S9, S10).
"The notice is void because the increase was calculated with rounding / compounding / the wrong year's rate."Decisive if trueProvide the worksheet: base rent, each year and rate, the sum, the exact product, the effective date.
"You raised our rent because we complained about repairs."Decisive if the facts fitA dated maintenance log and the 180-day bar in §1942.5; if a complaint exists, defer the increase until the window closes.
"You raised our rent because of who we are, or because we have a voucher."Decisive if the facts fitThe dated policy note plus identical methodology across units; FEHA covers source of income.
"You are trying to get us out without an eviction."SeriousNo vacate language, no buyout discussion in the same period, no reduction in services, and an increase within the ordinance's allowance.
"The increase was set by rent software."Serious under §37.10CShow a hand calculation from the published rate sheet.
"The notice arrived late."DecisiveProof of service, with mail extended by five days.

The documentation pack to assemble before service

  1. The lease for each unit showing the start date and initial rent.
  2. A rent ledger showing every payment and every increase — for these units, showing none.
  3. The Rent Board rate sheet (fact sheet 571) for the effective year, printed on the day of calculation.
  4. A one-page calculation sheet per unit: base rent × each available percentage, summed, then applied.
  5. Screenshot of the unit's rent-increase licence in the Rent Board Portal.
  6. The dated policy note describing how increases are decided across all units.
  7. The notice itself, with the annual/banked itemisation, and proof of service.
  8. A maintenance and complaint log covering the previous 12 months, to answer the §1942.5 question.

If all eight exist, a tenant-side reviewer has nothing to attack except the arithmetic — and the arithmetic is checkable in one step against the Board's published sheet.
