Ownership Footprint (Federal Statistical Context) Under the U.S. Census / HUD 2024 Rental Housing Finance Survey (RHFS), institutional entities (REITs, LPs, LLCs, corporations) overwhelmingly concentrate in 50+ unit buildings managed by professional third-party or in-house compliance teams. 2–4 unit residential buildings (like a 1940 2-unit Outer Sunset flat building) are predominantly owned by individual ('mom-and-pop') or family-trust owners self-managing without dedicated compliance staff. SF Admin. Code Ch. 37 applies identical legal rules to both — individual owners receive NO leniency on licensing, arithmetic, or service rules. Annual CPI Increases vs. Multi-Year Banking Automated property-management calendars issue the exact Form 571 annual CPI increase (e.g., 1.6%) every 12 months on schedule, preventing large banked backlogs and avoiding the 90-day (>10%) notice tier. Individual owners frequently leave rents unchanged for many years for stable tenants (as seen in Unit 2 since 2019) and later consider a multi-year banked catch-up — triggering 90/95-day notices and exposure to pending caps like File #261020. Adopting predictable, modest annual Form 571 adjustments (or disciplined partial banking) reduces tenant shock and legal scrutiny. San Francisco Rent Board (GS-10)Verbatim excerpt →
Housing Inventory (§ 37.15) & Rent Board Fee ($59.00) Centralized compliance staff batch-submit 5+ unit Housing Inventory templates and pay $59.00/unit fees before March 1 every year, storing timestamped license PDFs per unit. Most frequent mom-and-pop failure point: many small owners are unaware that 1–9 unit reporting began March 1, 2023, serving notices while unlicensed — rendering the increase unenforceable during suspension. Complete Individual Unit reporting on portal.sfrb.org and save both active license PDFs BEFORE serving any notice. San Francisco Rent Board (GS-02)Verbatim excerpt →
Pricing Software & Algorithmic Tools (§ 37.10C & DOJ v. RealPage) In non-rent-controlled markets, large operators widely used algorithmic revenue-management tools (e.g., RealPage, subject to DOJ's Nov. 24, 2025 proposed antitrust consent judgment). In SF, Admin. Code § 37.10C strictly BANS using algorithmic devices reliant on nonpublic competitor data. Individual owners rely on Form 571 for rent-controlled renewals and public listings for vacant units. Using prohibited algorithmic pricing software carries $1,000/month/unit penalties plus tenant attorney's fees. Never use prohibited algorithmic rent-setting tools; rely strictly on official Rent Board Form 571 percentages for existing tenants. San Francisco Rent Board (GS-31)Verbatim excerpt →
Above-Cap Cost Recovery (Capital Improvements & Passthroughs) Institutional landlords routinely file Rent Board Capital Improvement Petitions (§ 37.7), Operating & Maintenance Petitions, Water Revenue Bond passthroughs, and 50% Rent Board Fee ($29.50) offsets with CPA-grade documentation. Individual owners rarely file formal petitions unless performing major seismic/roof/system replacements, and sometimes mistakenly mix fee passthroughs into base rent (which is unlawful). Keep base-rent increases strictly separate from any optional $29.50 Rent Board Fee bill or utility passthrough. San Francisco Rent Board (GS-07)Verbatim excerpt →
