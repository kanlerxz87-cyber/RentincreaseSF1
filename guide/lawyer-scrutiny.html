1. Raising One Unit vs. Raising Both Units (Fair Housing & Discrimination Analysis)

How FEHA (Cal. Gov. Code § 12955), the Unruh Civil Rights Act (Civ. Code § 51), the Federal Fair Housing Act (42 U.S.C. § 3604), and SF Admin. Code § 37.10B apply to a 2-unit building.

Strategy Option Legal Risk Rating SF Tenant-Lawyer & Fair Housing Analysis Critical Operational Rule Official Source
Option 1 — Apply One Uniform, Objective Policy to BOTH Eligible Units (Recommended Best Practice) Lawful & Lowest Fair-Housing / Retaliation Litigation Risk In a 2-unit building, raising both eligible units under the exact same written rule (e.g., applying the published 1.6% Form 571 annual increase to each periodic tenancy upon eligibility) eliminates any 'comparator household' claim under FEHA (Gov. Code § 12955), Unruh (Civ. Code § 51), Federal FHA (42 U.S.C. § 3604), or SF § 37.10B(a)(9) that one household was singled out based on race, age, familial status, disability, sexual orientation, or source of income. NEVER raise an legally INELIGIBLE unit (e.g., if Unit 1 is mid-fixed-term lease) merely to force simultaneous notices — instead, document in writing that Unit 2 receives its eligible adjustment now and Unit 1 receives the identical formula on the date its fixed term expires. California Legislative Information (GS-29)Verbatim excerpt →
Option 2 — Raise Only Unit 2 Now & Defer Unit 1 Based on Documented Objective Lease/Tenure Facts Lawful IF Grounded Strictly in Objective, Non-Discriminatory Contract/Tenure Criteria Raising one unit is NOT automatically unlawful discrimination. If Unit 1's April 1, 2026 agreement is a 12-month fixed term (running through March 31, 2027) or is already near 2025 market ($3,995) while Unit 2 has had zero adjustments since June 1, 2019 ($3,150), an SF tenant lawyer will examine whether the distinction is objective and documented. If both units are month-to-month and legally eligible, selectively raising only one household without a contemporaneous, neutral written rationale creates avoidable evidentiary risk if the raised household belongs to a protected class or recently requested repairs. U.S. Government Publishing Office / California Legislative Information / SF.gov (GS-28)Verbatim excerpt →
Option 3 — Selective Increase Motivated by Protected Trait, Roommate Change, or Repair Request UNLAWFUL — Severe Statutory Damages & Fee-Shifting Exposure Selecting a unit because of a protected characteristic (FHA / FEHA / Unruh), because Tenant D moved into Unit 1 (§ 37.3(a)(11)), to pressure below-market tenants to vacate (§ 37.10B(a)(5)), or in retaliation for a habitability complaint (Civ. Code § 1942.5) exposes the owner to treble damages, statutory penalties, injunctions, and mandatory tenant attorney's fees. Never make oral or written remarks linking a rent increase to tenant demographics, occupancy changes, or repair costs. San Francisco Rent Board (GS-30)Verbatim excerpt →
