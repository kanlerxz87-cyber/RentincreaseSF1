Forefront Answer: Do Individual Property Owners Need a Rent Board License Before Raising Rent?

YES. Under San Francisco Administrative Code § 37.15 and SF Rent Board Housing Inventory rules, every owner of a rent-controlled unit — including an individual ("mom-and-pop") owner not in a corporation or LLC — must report each unit annually on the free Rent Board Portal to obtain a Rent Increase License. While the strict statutory gate requires holding the license on the increase's effective date, completing your Housing Inventory report and saving your active license PDF BEFORE serving the tenant notice is the essential best practice to prevent immediate tenant or attorney challenges. Standard annual/banked notices are served directly on the tenant (not filed with the Rent Board). Separately, leasing fewer than 4 residential units in a single building is conditionally exempt from the SF Treasurer's Business Registration Certificate.

Standard 1.6% annual increases are unaffected, but Unit 2's full 11.5% banked catch-up sits above 10%.

Do I submit the rent increase notice itself to the Rent Board or ask the Rent Board to approve the annual/banked increase? NO — Standard Annual & Banked Notices Go Only to the Tenant Once your unit's Housing Inventory is current and your Rent Increase License is active on portal.sfrb.org, you do NOT file standard annual allowable (1.6%) or banked (Rule 4.12) rent increase notices with the Rent Board, and you do NOT need to attach a copy of the license to the tenant's notice. Do I also need a San Francisco 'Business Registration Certificate' from the Treasurer & Tax Collector for a 2-unit residential building? CONDITIONAL NO — Exempt if Sole SF Business Activity Is Leasing <4 Residential Units in One Building Many individual owners confuse the Rent Board's Rent Increase License (REQUIRED) with the Treasurer & Tax Collector's Business Registration Certificate. Official SF Treasurer guidance states that a person receiving long-term residential rental income from 'one residential structure consisting of fewer than four units' is NOT, by reason of that fact alone, required to obtain a Business Registration Certificate or file Gross Receipts Tax returns — PROVIDED you have no short-term rentals (e.g., Airbnb), no SF payroll expense, and no other SF business activity. On the Rent Board Portal, the 'Business registration number, if any' field is optional when exempt. San Francisco Office of the Treasurer & Tax Collector (GS-04)Verbatim excerpt →
How does the annual SF Rent Board Fee ($59.00/unit for 2026–2027) relate to the free Rent Increase License? Requesting a fee exemption does NOT satisfy Housing Inventory reporting. After paying the $59.00 fee in full, you may optionally recover 50% ($29.50) from tenants in occupancy as of November 1 via security-deposit interest offset or a separate 30-day bill — never rolled into base rent.
