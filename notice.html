Notice &amp; Reasons

The exact sequence for a defensible notice, the contents it must carry, and which reasons survive review.

The notice is the whole case

If a San Francisco tenant disputes an increase, the landlord has to show the arithmetic, the licence and the service. The Ordinance makes a non-conforming increase void in its entirety, not merely reduced S08. That is why the checklist below is sequential: each step depends on the one before, and the most common failures are an unlicensed unit, a wrong-year rate, a missing banked-itemisation, and too short a notice period S01 S07 S09.

The banked-itemisation sentence nobody writes

Rule 4.12(b) requires the landlord to tell the tenant, on or before the date legal notice is given, “which portion of the rent increase reflects banked amount” and “the dates upon which said banked amount is based” S07. The rule softens the consequence — failure to include it “shall not render the increase null and void” — but in practice an un-itemised banked increase is the hardest thing to defend in a hearing, because the landlord then has to reconstruct the history from scratch under a burden of proof it carries.

Model sentence for a Unit B notice. “The monthly rent of $3,150.00 will increase by 11.7% ($368.55) to $3,518.55 effective [date]. Of this increase, 10.1% ($318.15) is a banked increase consisting of the annual allowable increases of 1.8% (effective 3/1/2020), 0.7% (3/1/2021), 2.3% (3/1/2022), 3.6% (3/1/2023) and 1.7% (3/1/2024) which were not imposed, and 1.6% ($50.40) is the current annual allowable increase effective 3/1/2026.” Figures are illustrative arithmetic from the published rates S03 and must not be sent until F01 and F04 are resolved.
