Notice & procedure

The nine steps in the order that keeps the increase enforceable, and the reasons that hold up.

Order matters. Two of these steps are void if done out of sequence: the licence must exist before the notice is enforceable, and any petition for an above-cap increase must be filed before the notice is served.

The compliant sequence

1

Confirm the lawful base rent for each unit from your own rent ledger

Why: Every calculation is a percentage of base rent. If a prior increase was never properly noticed, the lawful base rent is not simply the amount being collected.

The Rent Board can determine the lawful rent on petition and there is no time limit for a tenant to file (S20). Reconstruct each unit's increase history before calculating.

Before anything else S20

2

Confirm the anniversary date for each unit

Why: The anniversary date sets the earliest permissible effective date and which annual rate applies.

Anniversary date is either the date of the last increase or, if there has never been one, the date the lease began. For Unit 1 that is February 1 (S01). The April 1, 2026 change in named tenants does not obviously create a new anniversary date, because the rent did not change then.

Before anything else S01

3

Report into the Rent Board Housing Inventory and confirm the rent increase licence is active

Why: A notice is not enforceable without a licence in effect on the effective date.

The deadline is March 1 each year. A licence is generated automatically for non-owner-occupied units once you report. The licence does not need to be attached to the notice, but a tenant can look it up on the Rent Board Portal.

Before the notice is served; must be current on the effective date S10

4

Decide the amount: annual only, or annual plus banked

Why: This is the only lawful dial you control.

Unit 1: 1.6% ($63.92) or, if the February 1 anniversary applies, 3.0% ($119.85). Unit 2: up to 13.1% ($412.65) if effective on or before February 28, 2027.

Before drafting the notice S03 S04

5

Choose the service date so the notice period is satisfied

Why: The notice period runs to the effective date, not the mailing date.

Unit 1 at 3.0% or less needs 30 days (35 if mailed). Unit 2 at 13.1% needs 90 days (95 if mailed). Serve earlier than the minimum; there is no penalty for extra notice.

At least 30 or 90 days before the effective date S24

6

Serve a written notice that states every required element

Why: A missing element can make the increase void or unenforceable.

Required: the dollar amount, the percentage, the effective date, and which portion is annual versus banked (Sec. 37.3(b)(1)). Do not round up. Do not include passthroughs in the base. Serve by personal delivery or by mail per Code Civ. Proc. Sec. 1013.

After step 3, at least the required days before the effective date S08 S24

7

Keep a contemporaneous record of why the increase was made, and that it was applied even-handedly

Why: This is the evidence that defeats a discrimination or harassment claim.

The defensible reason is the legally available annual and banked increase, applied by a written policy. Record the date of the decision, the calculation, the notice served, and any communication with the tenant. If a tenant withholds rent or files a petition, the record is what a Rent Board ALJ or a court will read.

At the time of the decision S11

8

Consider offering the tenant information about free counselling and dispute resolution before a dispute exists

Why: It demonstrates good faith and often resolves the matter without a petition.

The Rent Board offers free counselling (415-252-4600) and a free Alternative Dispute Resolution program for housing-related disputes including rent increases. The City also publishes a list of free tenant counselling organizations.

With or shortly after the notice S34 S23

9

If the tenant files a petition, participate; do not retaliate

Why: Retaliation is a separate violation with its own remedies.

The Rent Board process is free and designed so no one needs an attorney. Filing a petition is not a basis for any adverse action, and a rent increase within 180 days of a tenantability complaint is separately prohibited by Civil Code Sec. 1942.5.

If and when a petition is filed S20 S27

A notice that would fail, and why

Unlawful

“Rent is increasing from $3,150 to $3,563 (13.1%) effective in 30 days, to bring the unit closer to market rent.”

  • 30 days' notice for an increase above 10% — defective. S24
  • "To bring the unit closer to market" is not a lawful basis and, on this record, is not even the real one. S14
  • No itemisation of the banked portion. S08

Defensible

“Effective February 28, 2027, base rent increases by $412.65 (13.1%), to $3,562.65. The increase consists of 1.6% annual allowable increase and 11.5% in previously banked annual increases (1.8% + 0.7% + 2.3% + 3.6% + 1.7% + 1.4%). Notice served 95 days in advance by mail.”

  • Correct notice period with the mailing allowance. S24
  • Itemised banked portion. S08
  • Every percentage reproduces from the Rent Board's own table. S03
