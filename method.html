Method

How the data was collected, verified and checked — and what remains uncertain.

This site was built backwards from its claims. The rule was: no sentence may assert a fact unless a numbered source in the source register supports it, and no table cell may contain a number that cannot be recomputed from a published figure.

The rules applied

One row, one source
Every table row carries its own source link, or is an arithmetic identity built from the published figures linked in the same row.
Blank beats guess
Where a figure depends on something unpublished (the 2027–28 rate) or unconfirmed (a market rent), the cell reads “unverified” and a flag explains what would resolve it.
Primary before secondary
Statutes, ordinances and agency pages only. Commercial sources are labelled and never used for a value. One commercial source was dropped from a claim when its own text did not support the claim.
Conflicts are recorded, not resolved silently
Where third-party sources contradicted the Rent Board, the conflict appears on the Flagged for review page rather than being quietly dropped.
Arithmetic is reproducible
Percentages are added, not compounded; the total is applied once to base rent; nothing is rounded up. Every figure was also recomputed independently by script.

What was checked, and how

  1. All 36 sources were fetched and read on 2026-11-06. The verification column on the Sources page states, per source, what it was used to confirm. Values were transcribed from the fetched text; none was recalled or inferred.
  2. The historical rate table was transcribed from the Rent Board's own PDF, cell by cell, and cross-checked against two independent Rent Board pages that publish the three most recent rates. S03 S19 S02
  3. Every dollar figure was recomputed by script from the percentages and the stated base rents. The checks are printed on the Calculations page.
  4. Every internal link and anchor on this site was checked after generation, and the site was confirmed to load as plain static files with no external JavaScript.
  5. Personal information was removed before anything was written, not after. No name map exists in this repository.

Known limits

  • This is an analysis of public law, not legal advice. A Rent Board counsellor's view on the Unit 1 anniversary question in particular would be worth obtaining before anything is served.
  • Every figure depends on the owner's own rent history being complete and accurate. That was not available and is flagged.
  • The building's coverage was accepted from the stated 1940 construction date and was not confirmed against Assessor or Planning records.
  • No market rent benchmark was obtained, and none is needed to compute a lawful ceiling.

How to check this yourself in five minutes

  1. Open the Rent Board's 571 Allowable Annual Increases PDF.
  2. For Unit 2, add the rates for every year since June 2019 that was skipped, then add 1.6%. The total should read 13.1%. Multiply by $3,150.
  3. For Unit 1, add 1.6% to $3,995. Optionally add the 1.4% year and multiply again.
  4. Compare with the Calculations page. If any number differs, treat that as a defect in this site and report it.
